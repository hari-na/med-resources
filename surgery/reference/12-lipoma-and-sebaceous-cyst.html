Short case 12 · General Surgery

Lipoma and Sebaceous Cyst

The commonest short case in any surgical examination, and the one where a complete answer takes ninety seconds. Two swellings, two signs, and one piece of vocabulary that Bailey & Love 28e says is wrong.

S Das: clinical method Bailey & Love: mechanism and management SRB: exam framework

1Before either diagnosis: the vocabulary

Swelling, mass and lump are not synonyms
SRB: "Swelling is an obvious enlargement of a part of the body due to various causes like congenital, inflammatory or neoplastic. Mass denotes a lesion which has difficulty to define the extent, e.g. mass abdomen; Lump denotes a defined lesion within an organ or the one causing the alteration in the gross nature or shape of a part or organ, e.g. breast lump."SRB 1 · p.68 Using the right word in your opening sentence costs nothing and sets the tone.
And the questions every swelling answers
Site, size, shape, surface, margin, consistency, fluctuation, transillumination, mobility, relation to skin and to deeper structures, and the regional nodes. For deciding whether a swelling is in the wall or deep to it, use the muscle-contraction test: an intra-abdominal mass becomes less prominent, a parietal one persists and becomes less mobile.SRB 16 · p.723

2Lipoma

What it is
SRB: "It is a benign neoplasm arising from yellow fat... composed of mature adipocytes and uniform nuclei that are identical to those seen in normal adult fat."SRB 1 · p.68
The three facts that make it quotable
"It is the most common benign tumour (karyotype 12q change). Prevalence is 2.1 per 100 people. It is called the universal tumour or ubiquitous tumour as it can occur anywhere in the body except brain."SRB 1 · p.68
And one more that explains a clinical observation"The fat in lipoma is considered unavailable for general metabolism."SRB 1 · p.68 So a lipoma does not shrink when the patient loses weight, which is why a starving patient can still have a large one.
Localised or diffuse, and why it matters surgically
Localised lipoma is encapsulated, yellowish-orange. Diffuse lipomas are not encapsulated and not localised, are common in palm, sole, head and neck, lie in subcutaneous and intermuscular tissues, and are "difficult to remove surgically. Recurrence is high after incomplete removal."SRB 1 · p.68 So the encapsulation is the whole difference between a five-minute excision and a recurrence.
The signs of a lipoma, and the one with a name SRB's description: soft, lobulated, non-transilluminant, mobile, with edges slipping between the palpating fingers, the slip sign. The skin is free. Lipomas may be pedunculated at times. It is rare in children.SRB 1 · p.69

The slip sign is the examinable one: press the edge of the swelling and the margin slips away from under your finger, because the lobulated fat is soft and the capsule thin. Lobulation is the second: feel across the surface and it is not smooth, because fibrous septa divide the fat into lobules. Together, lobulated and slipping, they are the diagnosis.

And it is rare in children, which is worth saying: a soft subcutaneous swelling in a child is more likely something else.

3Sebaceous cyst

Is "sebaceous cyst" even the right name? SRB 1 and S Das use it throughout; B&L 45 p.645 calls them epidermoid cysts and treats the name as colloquial
SRB and S Das
Use sebaceous cyst as the name of the entity throughout, and explain the punctum by the sebaceous duct: "Punctum is present over the summit in 70% of cases because here the sebaceous duct opens directly into the skin which gets blocked."SRB 1 · p.75
Bailey & Love 28e
Names them differently and gives a different origin: they are "derived from hair follicle infundibuli or traumatic inclusion. Commonly known as sebaceous cysts, they can occur anywhere. They are fixed to the skin and usually have a central punctum."B&L 45 · p.645 The phrase "commonly known as" is doing the work: the formal name is epidermoid cyst, and the cyst is a keratin-filled epidermal inclusion rather than a retained sebaceous secretion.
Both names describe the same lump, and the honest answer gives the clinical name first and then shows you know why it is a misnomer.

Say: "Clinically this is a sebaceous cyst. Strictly it is an epidermoid cyst, derived from hair follicle infundibula or from traumatic inclusion of epidermis, and it is filled with keratin rather than sebum, which is why 'sebaceous cyst' is regarded as a misnomer."B&L 45 · p.645

Then use the misnomer to explain a sign, which turns vocabulary into understanding: the punctum is black because of denuded squamous epithelium, that is keratin,SRB 1 · p.75 and keratin is an epidermal product, not a sebaceous one. The content of the cyst is the evidence that the name is wrong.

Do not refuse to say "sebaceous cyst" in an Indian examination: it is the term in both Indian texts and in the examiner's question.
The signs
SRB: soft or firm, smooth, free from deeper structures but fixed to the skin, fluctuant, with a positive Paget's test, non-transilluminating, with a punctum over the summit.SRB 1 · p.75
And the sign with a name"It moulds on finger indentation": the moulding sign.SRB 1 · p.75 Press it and it keeps the shape of your finger for a moment, because the contents are putty-like keratin rather than fluid.
The punctum, with the numbers
Present in 70% of cases, because there the duct opens directly into the skin and gets blocked. In 30% the duct opens into the hair follicle instead, so no punctum is seen. The punctum is a depressed, black-coloured spot over the summit, and it is black because of the denuded squamous epithelium, that is keratin.SRB 1 · p.75
SoAbsence of a punctum does not exclude the diagnosis, and that is a 30% caveat worth quoting.
Why the skin is fixed, and the lipoma's is not
Because the cyst arises from the skin, from a hair follicle infundibulum, so it cannot be separated from it;B&L 45 · p.645 a lipoma lies under the skin, so the skin moves freely over it.SRB 1 · p.69 That single difference separates the two diagnoses in one manoeuvre.
Hair loss over the surface
SRB: "Hair loss over the surface is common due to constant pressure over the roots of the hair follicles." And "unpleasant odour of sebum content is typical".SRB 1 · p.76 Both are visible or detectable without touching the patient.
The three complications, and the one with a famous name SRB: infection and abscess formation; surface ulceration;SRB 1 · p.76 and the two named sequelae.

Cock's peculiar tumour: when the cyst ulcerates it forms "a painful, fungating mass with discharge... often resembles epithelioma. It is a misnomer as it is not a tumour. It is a chronic granuloma on an ulcerated surface of a sebaceous cyst."SRB 1 · p.76 So it looks like a squamous carcinoma and is not one, which is exactly why it gets biopsied.

Sebaceous horn: "results from hardening of slowly discharged sebum through the punctum", a horn being something with greater length than breadth.SRB 1 · p.76

Two misnomers in one case: a cyst that is not sebaceous, and a tumour that is not a tumour.

Dermoid: SRB ch.1 p.76, which notes a dermoid is skin-free, has no punctum, often extends into a deeper plane or cavity through a suture line, commonly causes bone resorption and indentation, has restricted mobility, needs proper evaluation with radiograph or CT, and is excised under general anaesthesia.

The sentence that decides whether to image before you cut SRB on the dermoid: it "extends often into deeper planes or cavities through a suture line", bone resorption and indentation is common, mobility is restricted, and it "needs proper evaluation with X-ray or CT scan" with excision under general anaesthesia.SRB 1 · p.76

So the question to ask yourself before excising any midline or periorbital swelling in a child is: could this be a dermoid with an intracranial extension? If mobility is restricted or it sits over a suture line, image it first. That is the one way this short case can go badly wrong.

5Treatment

Lipoma
Excision, usually under local anaesthetic for a small localised one. The capsule makes enucleation easy. A diffuse lipoma is difficult to remove and recurrence is high after incomplete removal, so warn the patient and excise completely.SRB 1 · p.68
And one cautionA large, rapidly growing, deep or painful fatty swelling, particularly if intermuscular or retroperitoneal, must raise liposarcoma and be imaged and biopsied rather than simply shelled out.
Sebaceous cyst
Excision of the cyst with its wall intact, including the punctum, because any retained wall recurs. If infected, drain first and excise later when the inflammation has settled; SRB's complications are infection and abscess formation.SRB 1 · p.76
Cock's peculiar tumour
Because it resembles an epithelioma, biopsy it; it is a chronic granuloma on an ulcerated sebaceous cyst and is treated by excision once the diagnosis is secure.SRB 1 · p.76
Dermoid
Radiograph or CT first, then excision under general anaesthesia.SRB 1 · p.76

6Rapid-fire viva

How do you distinguish a lipoma from a sebaceous cyst in one manoeuvre?

Move the skin over the swelling. A sebaceous cyst is fixed to the skin, because it arises from a hair follicle infundibulum in the skin itself, and usually has a punctum; a lipoma lies beneath the skin, so the skin moves freely over it.B&L 45 · p.645SRB 1 · p.69, p.75 Then confirm with the lipoma's two signs: lobulated surface and a positive slip sign.

What is the slip sign?

Press the edge of the swelling and the margin slips away from under the palpating finger, because the lobulated fat is soft and the capsule thin. SRB describes a lipoma as soft, lobulated, non-transilluminant, mobile, with edges slipping between the palpating fingers, and the skin free.SRB 1 · p.69

Why is lipoma called the universal tumour?

Because it can occur anywhere in the body except the brain; SRB also calls it the ubiquitous tumour. It is the commonest benign tumour, with a karyotype 12q change, and a prevalence of 2.1 per 100 people.SRB 1 · p.68

Does a lipoma get smaller if the patient loses weight?

No. The fat in a lipoma is considered unavailable for general metabolism,SRB 1 · p.68 which is why a lipoma persists and may even look more prominent in a patient who has lost weight.

Which lipoma is difficult to remove?

The diffuse type, which is not encapsulated and not localised, common in palm, sole, head and neck, lying in subcutaneous and intermuscular tissue; it is difficult to remove surgically and recurrence is high after incomplete removal. The localised type is encapsulated and yellowish-orange and shells out easily.SRB 1 · p.68

Is "sebaceous cyst" the correct term?

Clinically yes, strictly no. Bailey and Love 28e calls these epidermoid cysts, derived from hair follicle infundibula or from traumatic inclusion, and says they are "commonly known as sebaceous cysts"; they are fixed to the skin and usually have a central punctum.B&L 45 · p.645 The contents are keratin rather than sebum, which is also why the punctum is black: SRB says it is black because of denuded squamous epithelium, that is keratin.SRB 1 · p.75 Use the clinical name and then show you know why it is a misnomer.

There is no punctum. Can it still be a sebaceous cyst?

Yes. The punctum is present in 70% of cases, where the duct opens directly into the skin and becomes blocked; in the other 30% the duct opens into a hair follicle instead, so no punctum is seen.SRB 1 · p.75

What is the moulding sign?

The cyst moulds on finger indentation, keeping the shape of the examining finger for a moment, because its contents are putty-like keratin rather than free fluid.SRB 1 · p.75 It sits alongside fluctuation, a positive Paget's test, and absent transillumination.

What is Cock's peculiar tumour?

An ulcerated sebaceous cyst that forms a painful fungating mass with discharge, closely resembling an epithelioma. It is a misnomer, because it is not a tumour at all but a chronic granuloma on the ulcerated surface of a sebaceous cyst.SRB 1 · p.76 Because it looks like a squamous carcinoma, biopsy it.

What is a sebaceous horn?

It results from hardening of slowly discharged sebum through the punctum, a horn being a projection with greater length than breadth.SRB 1 · p.76

Why is there hair loss over a scalp sebaceous cyst?

Constant pressure on the roots of the hair follicles. SRB also notes the unpleasant odour of the sebum content is typical.SRB 1 · p.76

A midline swelling over the bridge of the nose in a child. Would you just excise it?

No. Consider a dermoid cyst. SRB's features are skin free, no punctum, often extending into a deeper plane or cavity through a suture line, commonly with bone resorption and indentation, with restricted mobility, needing proper evaluation with radiograph or CT, and excision under general anaesthesia.SRB 1 · p.76 Restricted mobility or a position over a suture line means image before you cut, because of the possibility of deep or intracranial extension.

When would you worry about a fatty swelling?

If it is large, rapidly growing, deep, painful, intermuscular or retroperitoneal, raise liposarcoma and image and biopsy rather than shelling it out. Also note that lipoma is rare in children,SRB 1 · p.69 so a soft subcutaneous swelling in a child deserves a wider differential.

Distinguish swelling, mass and lump.

A swelling is an obvious enlargement of a part of the body, from congenital, inflammatory or neoplastic causes. A mass is a lesion whose extent is difficult to define, as in a mass per abdomen. A lump is a defined lesion within an organ, or one altering the gross shape of a part or organ, as in a breast lump.SRB 1 · p.68
