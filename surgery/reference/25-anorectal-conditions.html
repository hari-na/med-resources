Haemorrhoids, Fissure and Fistula-in-Ano

Three conditions, one examination, and one question that separates them before you touch the patient: is the bleeding painless or agonising, and is there a discharge? Painless bright bleeding is piles. Pain like passing glass is a fissure. Intermittent purulent discharge with a history of an abscess is a fistula. Everything else is detail.

2The examination, in the right order

Before you touch the patient The examiner is marking this before you have found anything. Bailey & Love gives it as a sequence: "ensure you have followed appropriate hand hygiene guidance. Introduce yourself and check the patient's name and date of birth. Request presence of a chaperone as appropriate. Explain what you are going to do, obtain verbal consent and ensure that the patient is comfortable."B&L 35 · p.472 And the standard it is held to: "patients should be treated with respect and dignity, receive a clear explanation of the examination undertaken and be kept as comfortable as possible. A chaperone should be present, especially for intimate examinations."B&L 21 · p.285

Do it, and be heard doing itWash your hands. Introduce yourself. Say what you want to examine and why, and ask permission. Ask for a chaperone where the examination is intimate. Expose what you need and cover the rest. Warm your hands. Tell the patient what each step will feel like and that they can ask you to stop, examine the tender part last, and watch their face rather than only your hands. Cover them and thank them before you turn to the examiner.

This one is intimate, so the chaperone is not a courtesy B&L's wording is "especially for intimate examinations", and it adds that this "should be part of a local guideline or policy".B&L 21 · p.285 Offer a chaperone whatever your sex and the patient's, say that you have offered, and say you would record it. For a child, explain it to the child and ask the parent. If consent is refused, say that you would explain again, offer a chaperone, document the refusal, and not proceed. Examiners ask what you would do if she declined, and that is the whole answer.

And for the rectal examination specifically B&L names the four things it needs before the finger goes anywhere: "it requires a relaxed patient who is informed of what the examination will entail, a private environment, a chaperone (for the security of both parties) and good light."B&L 80 · p.1419 Say all four aloud. 3Haemorrhoids

What they actually are, which has changed from what students are often taught
B&L: "haemorrhoids are symptomatic enlargements of the internal haemorrhoidal venous plexus."B&L 80 · p.1430 SRB puts the modern concept first: "it is downward sliding of anal cushions abnormally due to straining or other causes." And defines the cushions: "anal cushions (Thomson, 1975) are aggregation of blood vessels (arterioles, venules), smooth muscles and elastic connective tissue in the submucosa that normally reside in left lateral, right posterolateral and right anterolateral anal canal."SRB 25 · p.961
The sentence worth memorisingB&L: "the internal haemorrhoidal plexus constitutes the submucosal component of the anal cushions that are important in sealing the anal canal."B&L 80 · p.1430 Haemorrhoids are not varicose veins; they are normal structures that have slid down. That is why you do not excise all of them, and why continence can suffer if you do.
SRB adds "present concept is weakening of Park's ligament which is the lower end of the external sphincter", and the older sliding lining theory.SRB 25 · pp.961, 962
Why the blood is bright red, which is a favourite question
B&L explains it anatomically: there are "direct arteriovenous communications with the submucosal venous dilatations. These communications have been shown both histologically and radiologically, and the oxygen tension of the blood contained within the venous dilatations is more arterial than venous. This explains the bright red colour of haemorrhoid" bleeding.B&L 80 · p.1419 SRB's term for the same thing is "anal corpus cavernosum, by Stelzner".SRB 25 · p.962
So the answer is not "venous blood looks red in air"The blood in the anal cushions is arterialised through direct arteriovenous communications. SRB adds the extreme case: "an arterial pile is haemangiomatous condition of superior rectal artery entering the pedicle of internal haemorrhoid which will bleed profusely."SRB 25 · p.962
The positions, and why those positions
B&L: "internal haemorrhoids characteristically lie in the 3, 7 and 11 o'clock positions (with the patient in the lithotomy position). Secondary haemorrhoids may develop between the primary positions."B&L 80 · p.1430 SRB gives the reason: "located at 3, 7, 11 o'clock positions, related to the branches of the superior haemorrhoidal vessel which divides on the right side into two; left side it continues as one."SRB 25 · p.961
Say the vascular reason, not just the clock faceTwo branches on the right, one on the left. So right anterior (11), right posterior (7), left lateral (3). Always state the position of the patient, because the clock numbers are meaningless without it.
Internal, external and interno-external
SRB: internal, above the dentate line, covered with mucous membrane; external, below the dentate line, covered with skin; interno-external.SRB 25 · p.961 B&L: external haemorrhoids "relate to venous channels of the inferior haemorrhoidal plexus deep in the skin surrounding the anal verge and are frequently confused with anal skin tags that are not true haemorrhoids."B&L 80 · p.1430 And it calls the combined form "'mixed' haemorrhoids, which may be best considered as external extensions of internal haemorrhoids that arise through repeated congestion and oedema."B&L 80 · p.1430
The distinction that matters at the dentate lineAbove it, insensate visceral innervation; below it, somatic. That is why internal piles are painless and why a band or an injection placed below the dentate line causes severe pain, which is the next point.
The four degrees, on which both books agree
First: bleed only, no prolapse. Second: prolapse but reduce spontaneously. Third: prolapse and have to be manually reduced. Fourth: permanently prolapsed.B&L 80 · p.1430SRB 25 · p.961
Why the degree is the whole answer on treatmentBecause each degree maps to a treatment: first and second to injection or banding, third and fourth to operation. B&L's indications for haemorrhoidectomy are exactly that: "third- and fourth-degree haemorrhoids; second-degree haemorrhoids that have not been cured by non-operative treatments; 'mixed' haemorrhoids when the external haemorrhoid is well defined; bleeding causing anaemia."B&L 80 · p.1431
Complications
B&L's four: strangulation and thrombosis; ulceration; gangrene; portal pyaemia.B&L 80 · p.1430
How to manage a strangulated, thrombosed pileB&L gives both options and favours the conservative one: "urgent haemorrhoidectomy may expedite symptom resolution, but great care is needed to avoid later anal stenosis. Many surgeons adopt a conservative approach, ensuring adequate pain relief, bed rest, cold saline compresses and laxatives. Resolution usually occurs in 3 to 4 days. Systemic antibiotics are usually given to reduce the risk of portal pyaemia."B&L 80 · p.1430
Say the reason for the antibioticsPortal pyaemia, because the superior rectal veins drain to the portal system. That is the same anatomy that makes portal hypertension a rare cause of piles, which SRB lists.SRB 25 · p.962
Severe haemorrhage: B&L, "usually associated with a bleeding diathesis or anticoagulation. If such causes are excluded, a local compress containing adrenaline solution will usually suffice with blood transfusion if necessary."B&L 80 · p.1431
Conservative treatment
Both books: improve bowel and defaecatory habits, stool softeners and bulking agents, and a defaecatory position that minimises straining.B&L 80 · p.1431 SRB specifies: fibre 35 g a day, plenty of water, ispaghula husk, sterculia or methylcellulose, lactulose, and sitz baths, defined as sitting in warm water with the anal region submerged "for 20 minutes, 2 to 3 times a day".SRB 25 · p.963 SRB also suggests the squatting position may reduce the incidence of piles.SRB 25 · p.963

Injection sclerotherapy: the technique and the three errors

Rubber band ligation
Barron's bander, "used to slip tight elastic bands onto the base of the pedicle of each haemorrhoid." The rule: "it is essential that the band is applied above the dentate line as below can cause intense pain. The bands cause ischaemic necrosis of the piles, which slough off within 10 days; this may be associated with bleeding, about which the patient must be warned. The resulting fibrosis supports the remaining anal cushions."B&L 80 · p.1431

The operations
Open, Milligan-Morgan, "most commonly used in the UK": ligation and excision with the anal mucosa and skin left open to heal by secondary intention. Closed, Ferguson, "the popular technique in the USA": the wound is sutured.B&L 80 · p.1432
The single most important step, and the reasonLeave mucocutaneous bridges between the excised piles. B&L says it twice: "taking care to leave mucocutaneous bridges", and when injecting adrenaline, "to reduce bleeding and aid preservation of the skin bridges left following excision."B&L 80 · p.1432 Excise all the way round and you get an anal stenosis. That is why the operation removes three pedicles, not a ring of anal canal.
Technique: dilute adrenaline 1:300 000 into the anoderm; artery forceps on the skin-covered external component, traction to reveal the internal component; a V-shaped cut through the skin with scissors or cutting diathermy; dissection up the canal "with the internal sphincter visible and separate from the dissected pile"; a transfixion ligature on the pedicle, the pile excised well distal to the ligature, and the ligature cut long.B&L 80 · p.1432
Stapled haemorrhoidopexy (Longo), and transanal haemorrhoidal ligation (HAL), which is Doppler-guided ligation of the feeding vessels with or without suture mucopexy, used for second- and third-degree piles. B&L cites the trial: "the HubBLe trial, which compared HAL with rubber band ligation, found that the recurrence rate following HAL was significantly lower, but HAL was less cost-effective. The complication rate and postoperative pain scores are better after HAL than with conventional surgery."B&L 80 · p.1433 SRB's name for the same thing is Doppler-guided haemorrhoidal artery ligation (DGHAL).SRB 25 · p.963
On stapled haemorrhoidopexy, see the amber boxThe two books disagree about whether it works as well.
Post-operative care: B&L, "two warm baths each day", a bulk laxative twice daily, analgesia, and "a 5-day course of oral metronidazole may reduce pain"; review at 3 to 4 weeks with a rectal examination to look for stenosis.B&L 80 · p.1433
Anal dilatation: a useful manoeuvre, or an operation that has been abandoned? A live disagreement with a continence risk attached, and the most important one in this case. SRB 25 pp.963, 968 teaches Lord's dilatation and Recamier's stretching; B&L 80 p.1428 says it is rarely indicated
SRB
Teaches it for both conditions, with technique and finger counts. For oedematous prolapsed piles: "in case of inflamed, permanently prolapsed, oedematous piles, initially, manual stretching of the anal canal sphincter is tried. This prevents congestion of anal cushions and relaxes the anal sphincter, as a result of which the prolapsed piles gets reduced, Lord's dilatation (8 fingers). Once oedema subsides, in 1 to 2 weeks, formal procedure is done."SRB 25 · p.963 It lists "anal stretching, Recamier, Lord's" among the surgical options for haemorrhoids.SRB 25 · p.963 For an acute fissure: "stretching of the anal sphincter (Recamier, 1829) using two fingers of each hand (4 fingers) under anaesthesia is also an alternative one. Bailey & Love 28e
One sentence, under Operative measures for anal fissure, and it is a verdict: "anal sphincter dilatation has been used to reduce sphincter tone; however, this potentially disrupts the anal sphincters at multiple sites with an associated risk of incontinence such that it is rarely indicated."B&L 80 · p.1428 It does not appear at all among the treatments for haemorrhoids; B&L's summary box for haemorrhoid treatment lists only advice, injection, banding, HAL or stapled haemorrhoidopexy, and haemorrhoidectomy.B&L 80 · p.1433 B&L also lists dilatation among the causes of faecal incontinence.B&L 80 · p.1424
Which is right, and the specific point of dispute
B&L is right, and the disagreement is narrow and identifiable. Both books agree dilatation lowers sphincter tone and that it causes incontinence; they disagree on whether the incontinence is temporary. SRB says "usually temporary"; B&L's objection is structural, that an uncontrolled stretch tears the sphincter at multiple unpredictable sites, so the damage is neither measurable nor reversible, which is why B&L lists dilatation as a cause of incontinence rather than a treatment. The alternative that replaced it does the same job in a controlled way: a lateral internal sphincterotomy divides a known amount of one muscle in one known place, and chemical sphincterotomy with GTN, diltiazem or botulinum toxin lowers the tone reversibly. Note that SRB itself ranks the 4-finger stretch above the 8-finger one because complications are fewer, which is the same argument carried one step less far.
How to answer: know the eponyms, describe them accurately, and do not offer the operation. If asked what Lord's dilatation is: "manual dilatation of the anus under general anaesthesia using four fingers of each hand, described by Lord in 1969, to break the constricting band in the anorectal wall; Recamier's stretch, described in 1829, uses two fingers of each hand." Then, unprompted: "It is now rarely indicated, because an uncontrolled stretch disrupts the anal sphincters at multiple sites with a real risk of incontinence, and Bailey and Love lists anal dilatation among the causes of faecal incontinence. The controlled alternatives are chemical sphincterotomy with topical diltiazem or GTN, or botulinum toxin, and if those fail a lateral internal sphincterotomy, which divides a defined length of the internal sphincter in one known place."

For an Indian written paper, include it in the list where SRB does, name it, and add the continence caveat in the same breath. Volunteering the caveat is what distinguishes knowing the book from knowing the subject. Do not propose dilatation as your own management plan.
Stapled haemorrhoidopexy: equally effective, or higher recurrence with serious risks? A live disagreement about outcome. SRB 25 p.966 calls it "equally efficacious"; B&L 80 p.1433 says recurrence is higher and lists serious morbidity
SRB
"Stapled haemorrhoidopexy (Antonio Longo). It is circumferential excision of the mucosa and sub-mucosa 4 cm above the dentate line using circular haemorrhoidal stapler passed per anally... Advantages are, it is less painful; less blood loss; faster recovery; short hospital stay and equally efficacious. It is done only for prolapsed piles."SRB 25 · p.966 Its listed disadvantages are technical rather than outcome-related: "need for experience in advanced surgical skill; costlier; may cause a full-thickness excision of the rectal wall; may injure the anal sphincter. Improper purse string can cause incomplete doughnut leading to severe haemorrhage."SRB 25 · p.966
Bailey & Love 28e
Agrees on the benefits and then states the cost: "the procedure is less painful than conventional haemorrhoidectomy and is associated with quicker recovery. However, recurrence rates are higher than following conventional haemorrhoidectomy and external haemorrhoids may persist. Moreover, stapled haemorrhoidopexy has the potential for serious morbidity (staple line dehiscence, infection, rectovaginal fistula) and distressing new symptoms such as tenesmus (related to mucosal stimulation by the staples) may require reoperation and staple removal. Counselling and shared decision making is important such that the patient can weigh the short-term benefits against higher recurrence rates."B&L 80 · p.1433 It adds the two technical imperatives: "great care must be taken to ensure the staple line is above the dentate line and that the posterior vaginal wall is not accidently included."B&L 80 · p.1433
Where the difference comes from
Not the short-term data, on which both agree: less pain, quicker recovery. The difference is that B&L reports the long-term randomised evidence, which showed higher recurrence, and the particular complications that follow from stapling the rectum rather than excising the pile: a staple line that can dehisce, a rectovaginal fistula if the posterior vaginal wall is caught in the purse string, and tenesmus from the retained staples. Note also what the operation does and does not treat: it excises mucosa above the dentate line, so, as B&L says, the external component persists. SRB's own restriction, "it is done only for prolapsed piles", and its warning about full-thickness excision and sphincter injury are the same concerns at an earlier stage.
How to answer: describe the operation, then give the trade-off explicitly, because the trade-off is the answer. "Stapled haemorrhoidopexy, Longo's operation, uses a circular stapler to excise a cylinder of mucosa and submucosa above the dentate line, with the vessels within it, while stapling the ends together; it lifts the prolapsing cushions back rather than excising them, which is why it is used for prolapsing piles. It is less painful than a conventional haemorrhoidectomy and recovery is quicker, but recurrence rates are higher, any external component persists, and it carries specific risks: staple line dehiscence, infection, rectovaginal fistula if the posterior vaginal wall is caught in the purse string, and persistent tenesmus from the staples which can need reoperation. The staple line must be above the dentate line. So it needs proper counselling about short-term benefit against long-term recurrence." Do not say "equally efficacious" without the recurrence qualification.

Bailey & Love 28e
"All three primary haemorrhoids may be treated at one session, and the process may be repeated after several weeks." The band must be "above the dentate line".B&L 80 · p.1431 B&L also sets banding above injection: "for this reason, haemorrhoidal injection has largely been superseded by rubber band ligation."B&L 80 · p.1431

Why the numbers differ
The caution about banding more than one pile at a sitting is about pain and vasovagal reaction, not about efficacy; banding three pedicles at once gives a larger ischaemic area and more post-procedure discomfort. Practice genuinely varies, which is why one book says three and the other two. The 2 cm in SRB and B&L's "above the dentate line" are the same instruction with a margin of safety added, and on that there is no disagreement at all: a band below the dentate line sits on somatically innervated skin and causes intense pain.
How to answer: give the rule, not the count, and the rule is the thing being examined. "Rubber band ligation is used for first- and second-degree haemorrhoids that have not responded to conservative treatment. The band is applied to the base of the pedicle, well above the dentate line, about 2 cm above it, because a band on somatically innervated skin below the dentate line causes intense pain. Two bands per pedicle are often used in case one breaks. The pile undergoes ischaemic necrosis and sloughs off within one to two weeks, and the patient must be warned about bleeding at that point. The resulting fibrosis supports the remaining anal cushions. Depending on the patient's tolerance, one to three pedicles can be banded at a sitting, and the procedure is repeated after about three weeks if needed." If asked for a number in a written paper, follow SRB: two at a time, repeat after three weeks. The marks are in above the dentate line and in warning about secondary bleeding, not in the count.

4Fissure-in-ano

Definition
B&L: "an anal fissure (synonym: fissure-in-ano) is a longitudinal ulcer in the anoderm of the distal anal canal, which extends from the anal verge proximally towards, but not beyond, the dentate line."B&L 80 · p.1427 Every word earns its place: longitudinal, in the anoderm, distal, and it stops at the dentate line.
Why posterior, and why anterior in women
B&L is honest that it is not fully settled: "the cause of an anal fissure, and particularly the reason why the posterior midline is so frequently affected, is not completely understood. The location in the posterior midline may relate to the shearing forces acting at that site at defecation, combined with a less elastic anoderm endowed with an increased density of longitudinal muscle extensions in that region of the anal circumference."B&L 80 · p.1427 SRB gives the simpler mechanical version: "because of the curvature of the sacrum and rectum, hard faecal matter while passing down causes a tear in the anal valve leading to posterior anal fissure."SRB 25 · p.968
Anterior fissure: B&L, "more common in women and may arise following vaginal delivery."B&L 80 · p.1427 SRB: "anterior anal fissure is common in females due to lack of support to pelvic floor."SRB 25 · p.968
And why the position of the fissure matters clinically B&L: "a fissure that is not midline or one with atypical features should raise the suspicion of a specific aetiology."B&L 80 · p.1428 A lateral fissure is not a fissure until proven otherwise. It means Crohn's disease, tuberculosis, a sexually transmitted or HIV-related ulcer (B&L names syphilis, Chlamydia, chancroid, lymphogranuloma venereum, herpes simplex, cytomegalovirus, Kaposi's sarcoma, B-cell lymphoma) or a squamous carcinoma, and it needs examination under anaesthesia with biopsy and culture.B&L 80 · p.1428 SRB adds that "multiple fissures are seen in inflammatory bowel disease, homosexuals and venereal diseases."SRB 25 · p.968
The vicious cycle, which is the mechanism to recite
B&L: "affected patients frequently present with internal anal sphincter hypertonia, which, in turn, enhances the traumatic effect of the hard stool and perpetuates relative tissue ischaemia with a decrease in blood supply to the anal mucosa. After the initial tear, a vicious cycle of non-healing and repeated trauma leads to the development of chronic deep fissures. Local pain increases sphincter hypertonia, which worsens hard stool and local tissue ischaemia."B&L 80 · p.1428
Four links, and every treatment attacks one of them Hard stool → tear → pain → sphincter spasm → ischaemia → non-healing. Stool softeners break the first link, local anaesthetic and warm baths the third, and GTN, diltiazem, botulinum toxin or sphincterotomy the fourth. Say the cycle first and the treatments explain themselves.
Acute and chronic
Acute: SRB, "a deep tear in the lower anal skin with severe sphincter spasm without oedema or inflammation."SRB 25 · p.968
Chronic: the triad to describe. B&L: "chronic fissures are characterised by a hypertrophied anal papilla internally and a sentinel tag externally (both consequent on repeated healing and breakdown), between which lies an indurated anal ulcer that exposes fibres of the internal sphincter."B&L 80 · p.1428 SRB's version: inflamed indurated margins with scar tissue, a sentinel pile below which "acts like a guard", and a hypertrophied anal papilla above.SRB 25 · p.968
The three things to name in a chronic fissureSentinel tag below, indurated ulcer with visible internal sphincter fibres in between, hypertrophied papilla above. Seeing the white fibres of the internal sphincter in the floor is what makes it chronic.
And the paradox worth statingSRB twice: "pain is severe in nature in acute type, whereas less severe in chronic", and "chronic fissure is less painful than acute one."SRB 25 · p.968
Complications of a chronic fissure
SRB: "it can cause repeated infection, fibrosis, abscess formation, fistula formation."SRB 25 · p.968 B&L names the particular fistula: discharge may come "from an associated intersphincteric fistula, which has arisen through infection penetrating via the fissure base."B&L 80 · p.1428 So a fissure can become a fistula, which is why these three conditions belong in one case.
Treatment: conservative first, and it works
B&L: "after confirmation of the diagnosis and exclusion of secondary causes of anal ulceration, conservative management should result in the healing of almost all acute and the majority of chronic fissures. Emphasis must be placed on normalisation of bowel habits. The addition of fibre to the diet to bulk up the stool, stool softeners and adequate water intake are simple and helpful measures. Warm baths and topical local anaesthetic agents relieve pain."B&L 80 · p.1428 SRB's list is the same: 6 to 8 glasses of fluid, fibre-rich diet, psyllium husk or bran, lactulose, 5% lignocaine, sitz baths.SRB 25 · p.968
B&L adds one more for the right patient: "patients with normal bowel function and excessive straining at defecation might benefit from anorectal biofeedback to correct it."B&L 80 · p.1428
Chemical sphincterotomy, with the doses
B&L: "the mainstay of current conservative management is the topical application of pharmacological agents that relax the internal sphincter... glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) (0.2% applied two or three times per day to the anal margin) is a nitric oxide donor while diltiazem (2% applied twice daily) is a calcium channel antagonist. Botulinum toxin (10 to 100 units) injected into the internal sphincter in either divided or a single dose reduces anal canal pressure by blocking the release of acetylcholine at neuromuscular junctions. Temporary incontinence occurs in up to 10% of patients. The cure rate is approximately 50%, although GTN can be associated with headaches, which limits its acceptability to patients. Diltiazem and botulinum toxin have similar efficacy with fewer side effects."B&L 80 · p.1428
SRB agrees, and gives a specific botulinum dose: "botulinum toxin 25 units injected into the internal sphincter. It causes temporary denervation of the internal sphincter; reducing its tone, improving the blood supply and control of ischaemia. It causes temporary incontinence for flatus (10%)." And on GTN: "topical nitroglycerine 0.2% is also used to relax the sphincter. It causes severe headache."SRB 25 · p.969 SRB also lists 2% diltiazem, L-arginine and 2% nifedipine ointment.SRB 25 · pp.968, 969
Prefer diltiazem, and say whySame efficacy, no headache. Both books say it. GTN's headache is the reason patients stop using it, which is why the cure rate in practice is lower than the pharmacology promises.
Lateral internal sphincterotomy: the operation and its price
Where: "the internal sphincter is divided away from the fissure itself, usually either in the right or the left lateral positions."B&L 80 · p.1428 SRB: the same, "either in right or left lateral positions", with open or closed methods (Notaras), and "sphincterotomy is done below the dentate line", dividing the lower third or half of the internal sphincter.SRB 25 · p.969
Closed method: a small longitudinal incision at the intersphincteric groove, planes developed, and precise division of the internal sphincter "to the level of the apex of the fissure"; or a number 11 blade advanced parallel to the sphincter and then rotated so the sharp edge faces it. Pressure is applied for a few minutes to prevent a haematoma.B&L 80 · p.1428
Open method: the anoderm over the distal internal sphincter is divided longitudinally, the sphincter divided, and the wound closed with absorbable sutures.B&L 80 · p.1428
The fissure itself needs nothing: "although the fissure needs no specific attention, problematic papillae and external tags can be excised."B&L 80 · p.1428 That is the whole logic of the operation: you treat the sphincter, not the ulcer.
The numbers to quote, and they are the reason conservative treatment comes firstB&L: "healing rates are in the range of 85%, but there is also a significant risk of altered continence (9% flatus incontinence, 6% soiling, under 1% solid stool incontinence)."B&L 80 · p.1429 Early complications: haemorrhage, haematoma, bruising, perianal abscess and fistula.B&L 80 · p.1429 SRB's list is the same.SRB 25 · p.969 And B&L concedes: "the optimal amount of sphincter to be divided is a matter of debate, and additional factors have to be considered such as patient age, sex, previous vaginal delivery and operations on the anal canal."B&L 80 · p.1428
Fissurectomy and the advancement flap, for the patient you must not sphincterotomise
Fissurectomy: "surgical excision of a fissure involves excising of the fibrotic edge, curettage of the base and excision of the sentinel tag and/or anal papilla. Fissurectomy is an alternative to lateral internal sphincterotomy and is used if there are contraindications to lateral internal sphincterotomy. It is frequently combined with an advancement flap anoplasty."B&L 80 · p.1429 Note what it does not include: a sphincterotomy.
Anal advancement flap: "should be considered in those with an increased risk of altered continence following lateral internal sphincterotomy, especially in postpartum women and those with normal or low resting anal pressures." An inverted house-shaped flap of perianal skin is mobilised on its blood supply and advanced without tension to cover the fissure.B&L 80 · p.1429 SRB agrees: "anal advancement flap is more effective especially in females."SRB 25 · p.969
The patient this is for, in one sentenceA postpartum woman with a normal or low resting anal pressure. She has nothing to spare: her sphincter is already damaged and her resting pressure is not high, so the fissure is not from hypertonia and dividing muscle will make her incontinent. Identifying that patient is the single best thing you can say about fissure surgery.
Two conditions to be able to name alongside
Hypertrophied anal papilla. B&L: papillae "occur at the dentate line and are remnants of the ectodermal membrane that separated the hindgut from the proctodaeum. As these papillae are present in 60% of patients examined proctologically, they should be regarded as normal structures." They elongate in the presence of a fissure, and a red oedematous papilla with purulent discharge from its crypt is cryptitis, "cured by laying open the mouth of the infected anal gland and excising the papilla."B&L 80 · p.1429
Proctalgia fugax. "Attacks of severe pain arising in the rectum, recurring at irregular intervals and apparently unrelated to organic disease. The pain is described as cramp-like, often occurring at night, lasting minutes and disappearing spontaneously. It seems to occur more commonly in patients suffering from anxiety or undue stress."B&L 80 · p.1429 SRB lists it among the differential diagnoses of a fissure.SRB 25 · p.968
Dorsal fissurectomy with sphincterotomy in the floor of the fissure: is that still an operation? A live disagreement, and the point of lateral sphincterotomy is at stake. SRB 25 pp.968, 969 offers it for chronic fissure; B&L 80 p.1429 describes fissurectomy without any sphincterotomy and places its sphincterotomy deliberately laterally
SRB
Lists it first for chronic fissure: "dorsal fissurectomy with sphincterotomy is done under anaesthesia. Specimen should be sent for biopsy to rule out carcinoma, tuberculosis, etc. Here transverse fibres of internal sphincter is divided in the floor of the fissure."SRB 25 · pp.968, 969 It then gives lateral anal sphincterotomy as a second option which "also gives a good result".SRB 25 · p.969
Bailey & Love 28e
Has both components, but never in the same place. Its fissurectomy is excision of the fibrotic edge, curettage of the base and excision of the tag and papilla, with no sphincter division at all, used "if there are contraindications to lateral internal sphincterotomy" and "frequently combined with an advancement flap anoplasty".B&L 80 · p.1429 Its sphincterotomy is defined by being somewhere else: "the internal sphincter is divided away from the fissure itself, usually either in the right or the left lateral positions", and "although the fissure needs no specific attention", tags and papillae may be excised.B&L 80 · p.1428 A posterior midline sphincterotomy appears nowhere.
Why the position of the cut is the whole argument
Dividing the internal sphincter in the posterior midline, in the floor of the fissure, was the original operation, and it was abandoned for a specific named reason: it leaves a keyhole deformity, a posterior midline gutter which heals slowly and through which mucus and liquid stool leak, causing soiling and pruritus. Moving the same cut laterally gives the same reduction in sphincter tone in healthy tissue that closes properly, leaves no gutter, and lets the fissure heal on its own once the spasm and ischaemia are relieved, which is why B&L can say the fissure needs no attention. SRB does not dispute the physiology; it simply still lists the older operation, and it does recommend sending the specimen for histology, which is a real advantage of excising the fissure.
How to answer: offer the lateral operation and name the reason the midline one was abandoned, which is the mark. "For a chronic fissure that has failed conservative and chemical treatment, I would do a lateral internal sphincterotomy, dividing the lower third of the internal sphincter in the right or left lateral position, away from the fissure. The fissure itself needs no treatment, though a sentinel tag or hypertrophied papilla can be excised. The reason the sphincterotomy is lateral rather than in the floor of the fissure, which is how it was originally done, is that a posterior midline division heals as a keyhole deformity, a gutter through which mucus and liquid stool leak. Where a sphincterotomy is contraindicated, in a postpartum woman or a patient with a normal or low resting anal pressure, I would do a fissurectomy with an advancement flap instead." If an Indian paper asks for the operations for chronic fissure-in-ano, list SRB's both, dorsal fissurectomy with sphincterotomy and lateral anal sphincterotomy, and say that the lateral operation is preferred, with the keyhole reason. Always add that the excised specimen goes for histology, which is SRB's own point and a good one.

5Anorectal abscess, which comes before the fistula

Most anorectal abscesses are the acute phase of the same disease as the fistula, and the abscess is the one that arrives as an emergency. Not all of them are, and B&L says so in the same breath as the theory: "perianal infections with skin-type organisms (and thus unrelated to fistula) are evenly distributed" between the sexes.B&L 80 · p.1435 So a perianal abscess is not automatically a fistula in waiting. B&L's account of the ones that are rests on Parks' cryptoglandular theory: infection starts in an intersphincteric anal gland and "pus, which travels along the path of least resistance, may spread caudally to present as a perianal abscess, laterally across the external sphincter to form an ischiorectal abscess or, rarely, superiorly above the anorectal junction to form a supralevator intermuscular or pararectal" abscess.B&L 80 · p.1435 That single sentence gives you the classification and the anatomy of spread together, which is why it is worth learning verbatim.

The treatment, which is drainage and antibiotics
B&L: "management of acute anorectal sepsis is primarily surgical, including careful examination under anaesthesia, sigmoidoscopy and proctoscopy, and adequate drainage of the pus."B&L 80 · p.1436 Its summary box gives the whole of it in one line, and the second half is the half students leave out: "treatment is drainage of pus and appropriate systemic antibiotics."B&L 80 · p.1436 SRB lists the same supporting measures: "sitz bath, antibiotics, analgesics" alongside "drainage under G/A".SRB 25 · p.970
Never answer this one with drainage aloneSay drainage of pus with appropriate systemic antibiotics. Surgical drainage is the primary treatment and antibiotics do not replace it, but the book pairs them, and an answer that omits antibiotics is incomplete against B&L's own summary.
Where you drainFor perianal and ischiorectal sepsis, which B&L puts at 60% and 30% of cases, drainage is through the perineal skin.B&L 80 · p.1436 SRB gives the same incision for the ischiorectal abscess: cruciate.SRB 25 · p.970
The supralevator abscess is the exception, and it is a trap It is the one named in the classification above that is not simply drained through the perineum. B&L: treatment depends on the underlying cause "(appendiceal, gynaecological, diverticular, Crohn's disease, malignancy), although intrarectal drainage may be appropriate to avoid creation of an extrasphincteric fistula."B&L 80 · p.1436 Draining a supralevator collection through the perineum takes the track across the whole sphincter complex and makes the extrasphincteric fistula that is hardest of all to cure.
And the investigationSRB: "MRI is the investigation of choice for anorectal abscess."SRB 25 · p.970 It is what settles a deep or recurrent collection you cannot define clinically.
How much skin to take, where B&L qualifies the traditional answer
"Traditionally this has been through a cruciate incision over the most fluctuant point, with excision of the skin edges to deroof the abscess; however, although drainage must be ensured, skin preservation is important and wide excision of otherwise healthy tissue should be avoided."B&L 80 · p.1436
Say both halvesName the cruciate incision, then add that current teaching is to preserve skin rather than excise widely. That is the book's own sentence, so it cannot be argued with.
The two things B&L tells you not to do
These are the marks in this section, and they are stated as prohibitions:
1. "Injudicious probing in the acute stage is, however, potentially dangerous and may lead to a much more difficult situation."B&L 80 · p.1436
2. "Unless by highly experienced hands, immediate fistulotomy should not be performed."B&L 80 · p.1436
What you may do instead"A gentle search may be made for an underlying fistula if the surgeon is experienced; if obvious, a loose draining seton may be passed."B&L 80 · p.1436 Note loose, and note draining: this is not the cutting seton of the elective fistula operation below, and the distinction is worth making out loud.
The ischiorectal abscess that does not fluctuate
SRB's warning is the one that prevents a missed diagnosis: it "presents with tender, indurated, brawny swelling in the skin over the ischiorectal fossa with high fever. Swelling is not well-localised and fluctuation is absent in ischiorectal abscess."SRB 25 · p.970
So waiting for fluctuation is the errorA deep abscess in a large fatty space will not fluctuate before it has done harm. Fever and brawny induration are enough to take the patient to theatre.
And what the abscess may be telling you
B&L ends its summary with the list to consider underneath: "fistula-in-ano, Crohn's disease, diabetes, immunosuppression."B&L 80 · p.1436
Worth one sentence in the vivaA recurrent or atypical perianal abscess is a reason to look for Crohn's disease and to check the blood sugar, not simply to drain it again.
And in these patients it is not a minor operationB&L: "immunosuppressed patients or those with diabetes or acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) may present with perianal or pelvirectal sepsis that may run an aggressive course."B&L 80 · p.1435 That is the sentence behind necrotising perineal sepsis, and it is why a diabetic with perianal pain is drained early and watched rather than sent home on tablets.
Packing
B&L is candid that this is habit rather than evidence: "despite lack of evidence, the practice of packing the abscess cavity is commonplace."B&L 80 · p.1436 If you are asked, say that it is common practice and that the book records there is no evidence behind it. Note the wording: lack of evidence is not evidence against, and saying the book has shown packing to be wrong goes further than the book does.

6Fistula-in-ano

Definition and cause
B&L: "a fistula-in-ano, or anal fistula, is a chronic abnormal communication extending from the anorectal lumen (the internal opening) to an external opening on the skin of the perineum or buttock (or rarely, in women, to the vagina). The majority are idiopathic or cryptoglandular and are lined by granulation tissue."B&L 80 · p.1436
SRB gives the figure: "95% of anorectal abscesses are due to infection of anal glands in relation to crypts, cryptoglandular disease."SRB 25 · p.969 It names the glands: the cryptoglands of Morgagni.SRB 25 · p.972
Say the word cryptoglandular, and what it meansAn anal gland in the intersphincteric space becomes infected through its crypt at the dentate line; the abscess tracks out to the skin; the resulting track is the fistula. That is why the internal opening is at the dentate line, and why curetting the infected gland is part of the operation. SRB: "proper curetting of the infected anal gland area is essential."SRB 25 · p.975
The other causes, which are the ones that make the fistula complex
B&L: Crohn's disease, tuberculosis, lymphogranuloma venereum, actinomycosis, rectal duplication, foreign body and malignancy, which "may also very rarely arise within a longstanding fistula."B&L 80 · p.1436 SRB adds ulcerative colitis, hidradenitis suppurativa and trauma,SRB 25 · p.972 and explains the Crohn's association anatomically: "plenty of lymphoid aggregates surround the anal glands, which explain the high incidence of anal fistula in Crohn's disease."SRB 25 · p.972
And how a tuberculous fistula differs on examinationSRB: "tuberculous fistulas do not have induration, will have pale granulation tissue with watery discharge and they are most often multiple."SRB 25 · p.974 Multiple openings with no induration and watery discharge should send you looking for tuberculosis, Crohn's, ulcerative colitis, lymphogranuloma venereum, hidradenitis or actinomycosis, which is SRB's own list for multiple openings.SRB 25 · p.972
Presentation
B&L: "patients usually complain of intermittent purulent discharge (which may be bloody) and discomfort, which increases until temporary relief occurs when the pus discharges. There is often a history of anorectal sepsis."B&L 80 · p.1436
One question that localises the internal opening without any instrument B&L: "the passage of flatus or faeces through the external opening is suggestive of a rectal rather than an anal internal opening."B&L 80 · p.1436
SRB's figures: prevalence 0.01%, commoner in young adult males, 2 to 1, with seropurulent discharge in 65%; and the natural history, "often it may heal superficially but pus may collect beneath forming an abscess which again discharges through same or new opening."SRB 25 · p.973
Classification: Park's, which is the one that decides management
B&L: "the most widely used classification of anal fistulae (Parks') is based on anal gland sepsis in the intersphincteric space (the internal opening is at the dentate line); this results in a primary track whose relation to the external sphincter defines the type of fistula, which influences management."B&L 80 · p.1436
Intersphincteric: does not cross the external sphincter; "most commonly they run directly from the internal to the external openings across the distal internal sphincter", and may extend proximally in the intersphincteric plane.B&L 80 · pp.1436, 1437
Trans-sphincteric: "a primary track that crosses both internal and external sphincters (the latter at a variable level) and that then passes through the ischiorectal fossa to reach the skin of the buttock." Secondary tracks may reach the roof of the ischiorectal fossa or rarely through the levator into the pelvis, and "circumferential (horseshoe) spread of sepsis may occur."B&L 80 · p.1437
Suprasphincteric: "run up to a level above the puborectalis and then curl downwards through the levators and ischioanal fossa to reach the skin. They are often caused by excessive probing of an abscess cavity or fistula tract during examination under anaesthesia."B&L 80 · p.1437
Extrasphincteric: "run without specific relation to the sphincters and usually result from pelvic disease or trauma."B&L 80 · p.1437
Note what B&L says causes a suprasphincteric fistula Excessive probing. It is iatrogenic. Which is why SRB insists that "probing is done under general anaesthesia gently with care without creating extensions",SRB 25 · p.973 and why probing in the ward is not acceptable.
On the percentages, see the amber boxThe two books give different figures for how common each type is.
And the two older classifications SRB expects by name
Standard (Milligan-Morgan 1934, Goligher 1975): subcutaneous (commonest), low anal (common), submucous, high anal, pelvirectal.SRB 25 · p.972
High and low: "low level fistulas, these open into the anal canal below the internal ring. High level fistulas, these open into the anal canal at or above the internal ring."SRB 25 · p.972 This is the division SRB organises its treatment around, and it is the practical one: low fistulas can be laid open, high ones cannot.
Simple and complex, which is the division that decides whether you refer
B&L uses the American Gastroenterology Association classification, "which condenses the Parks' classification into simple and complex fistula" and is "helpful in the decision to operate on clinical findings, investigate further or refer for specialist opinion."B&L 80 · p.1436
Simple: low, meaning superficial or low inter- or low trans-sphincteric, with a single external opening.
Complex: high inter- or trans-sphincteric; extra- or suprasphincteric; presence of an abscess or collection; ano-vaginal fistula; anal stricture.B&L 80 · p.1436
SRB's definition of complex is worth having too, because it adds the patient factors: "the track crosses over 50% of the external sphincter, anterior in females, multiple tracks, recurrent, or the patient has preexisting incontinence, local irradiation, or Crohn's disease."SRB 25 · p.972
Why "anterior in females" is on that listBecause the anterior sphincter is short in a woman and may already be damaged by childbirth, so there is far less muscle to spare. The same reasoning as the advancement flap in fissure.
Clinical assessment: the five things to establish
B&L: "the site of the internal opening; the site of the external opening(s); the course of the primary track; the presence of secondary extensions; and the presence of other conditions complicating the fistula."B&L 80 · p.1437
What induration tells you: "palpable induration between the external opening and the anal margin suggests a relatively superficial track, whereas supralevator induration suggests a primary track above the levators or high in the roof of the ischiorectal fossa, or a high secondary extension. Intersphincteric fistulae usually have an external opening close to the anal verge."B&L 80 · p.1437
Finding the internal opening: it "may be felt as a point of induration or seen as an enlarged papilla. Dilute hydrogen peroxide, instilled via the external opening, can demonstrate the site of the internal opening; gentle use of probes and a finger in the anorectum usually delineates primary and secondary tracks and their relations to the sphincters."B&L 80 · p.1437 SRB uses methylene blue on the table for the same purpose.SRB 25 · p.975
Investigations
MRI is the gold standard. B&L: "MRI is the 'gold standard' for fistula imaging. Short tau inversion recovery (STIR) sequencing (a fat-suppression technique) to highlight the presence of pus and granulation tissue without the need for contrast medium has been revolutionary. The great advantage of MRI is its ability to demonstrate secondary extensions, which may be missed at surgery and cause persistence."B&L 80 · p.1438 SRB agrees: "MRI or MRI fistulogram ideal."SRB 25 · p.974
Say why MRI and not fistulographyBecause the thing that makes a fistula recur is a secondary extension you did not find. A fistulogram shows the track you already know about; MRI shows the one you do not.
Endoanal ultrasound: B&L, "EAUS, especially with hydrogen peroxide instilled through the external opening, is more accurate than clinical examination and is useful to determine whether a fistula is simple or complex", and it assesses sphincter integrity.B&L 80 · p.1438
Fistulography and CT are "useful if an extrasphincteric fistula is suspected."B&L 80 · p.1438
And for the cause: SRB, chest radiograph, ESR, biopsy, discharge study, and colonoscopy "often when ulcerative colitis or Crohn's is suspected".SRB 25 · p.974
SRB's warning about probing in the ward"Probing in the ward and fistulogram in the ward before surgery using Lipiodol is not advisable as it may cause recrudescence of inflammation."SRB 25 · p.973
The principle of fistula surgery, which is one sentence and one number
B&L: "the aim of surgery is to keep the patient continent and comfortable and whenever possible to eradicate the fistula." And: "division of any component of the sphincter mechanism carries some risk to continence. The most important determinant of function after fistulotomy is the amount of muscle left behind rather than that divided. In the presence of a normal bowel habit, continence is usually maintained as long as a minimum length of external sphincter is retained (2 cm as a rule but less in some cases)."B&L 80 · p.1439
That is the answer to almost any fistula questionContinence first, cure second, and 2 cm of external sphincter is the number. Note the order of the aims: continent comes before comfortable, and both come before eradicating the fistula. B&L also notes that "patients with minimal symptoms may be managed expectantly."B&L 80 · p.1439
The operations for a low fistula
Fistulotomy, laying the track open to heal by secondary intention, which B&L notes "has been practised for centuries and was beautifully described by John of Arderne".B&L 80 · p.1438 SRB: "it is done in low anal fistula. It is technically easier. After passing the probe through the entire fistulous track, incision is made over the probe to cut and lay open the fistulous track. It is allowed to granulate and heal from the floor or surface. Technique is safer, easier and can be done on outpatient basis."SRB 25 · p.975
Fistulectomy, excising the track. SRB: probe through to the internal opening, the fistula opened along the probe, then "fibrous track along with unhealthy granulation tissue and additional external openings are excised. Specimen is always sent for histopathology." And: "fistulectomy for low level fistulas do not cause rectal incontinence."SRB 25 · pp.974, 975
Fistulotomy or fistulectomy?Lay open rather than excise where you can. Fistulotomy removes less tissue, heals faster and risks less sphincter; fistulectomy gives you a specimen for histology, which matters where tuberculosis, Crohn's or malignancy is possible. Saying that trade-off is better than preferring one blindly.
The sphincter-sparing options, which SRB lists more fully than B&L
Seton. B&L notes the etymology and the history: "the use of a seton (from the Latin seta, meaning a bristle) to drain fistula tracts and gradually deliver the tract to the surface has a long history, most famously used by Charles Felix to treat French King Louis XIV."B&L 80 · p.1439 SRB gives the mechanism and the duration: the muscle superficial to the track is encircled and the seton "tied securely and left in situ to create ischaemic necrosis, dividing the muscle slowly without allowing it to spring apart avoiding gutter deformity... it will well-preserve the sphincter function and pressure." It "can be kept for 3 months" and replaced "by rail road technique without anaesthesia"; it is "staged fistulotomy".SRB 25 · p.975
Why a seton works, in one lineIt divides the muscle slowly so the cut ends scar together instead of springing apart. That is the same gutter-deformity argument as the lateral sphincterotomy, and it is why a seton preserves continence where a single cut would not.
Advancement flap, a mucosal flap procedure, used occasionally.SRB 25 · p.975
Fibrin glue: "a multicomponent system containing mainly human plasma fibrinogen and thrombin. Once prepared it is injected into the fistula track which hardens in few minutes and fills the entire track. Success rate is 70%." SRB also notes glue results are "not good" in its own earlier sentence.SRB 25 · p.975

What B&L's qualification actually says
It is subtle and worth unpacking, because it is not a rejection. Goodsall's rule predicts that an anterior external opening connects by a straight radial track to an internal opening at the nearest point of the dentate line, and that a posterior one curves to the posterior midline. B&L is saying that the posterior half of the rule holds well, but the anterior half does not: an anterior external opening often also leads to a midline internal opening rather than to a radially adjacent one, so following a straight radial line can take you to the wrong place. Note that SRB's own text, two sentences later, agrees with the part B&L keeps: "P/R examination shows indurated internal opening usually in the midline posteriorly" and "most of the fistulas are on posterior half of anus."SRB 25 · p.973 So the books differ in emphasis rather than in observation, and the practical consequence is that you confirm the internal opening rather than predicting it: hydrogen peroxide or methylene blue through the external opening, gentle probing under anaesthesia, and MRI where there is doubt.B&L 80 · p.1437SRB 25 · p.975
How to answer: state the rule, use it, and say what you would do to confirm it. "Goodsall's rule: an external opening in the anterior half of the anus usually connects by a straight radial track to an internal opening at the nearest point of the dentate line, whereas an opening in the posterior half usually has a curved track opening in the posterior midline, which is why posterior fistulae may be horseshoe-shaped with several external openings sharing one internal opening. It is a guide rather than a rule: Bailey and Love points out that the majority of internal openings are midline in both the anterior and posterior planes, so I would confirm the internal opening rather than assume it, by instilling dilute hydrogen peroxide or methylene blue through the external opening, by gentle probing under anaesthesia, and by MRI where the anatomy is in doubt or the fistula is recurrent." Both books accept the rule for posterior openings, so you can use it with confidence there and hedge anteriorly.

Why they differ, and what both agree on
These are referral-pattern figures, not biology. A specialist unit that receives recurrent and complex fistulae, which is where B&L's data come from, will see proportionally more trans-sphincteric, supra- and extrasphincteric disease, so its intersphincteric share falls. A general surgical series sees mostly straightforward low fistulae. Neither set is wrong for its own population. What both books agree on is the only part that affects your answer: intersphincteric is commonest, trans-sphincteric next, and the two together account for the great majority; supra- and extrasphincteric fistulae are rare and are the ones that need specialist management. B&L adds that suprasphincteric fistulae are often iatrogenic, from excessive probing,B&L 80 · p.1437 which is itself a reason the rarer types appear more often in a tertiary series.

The clinically important point, which neither book disputes, is that the relation of the track to the external sphincter is what decides whether the fistula can be laid open, and supra- and extrasphincteric fistulae cannot be.

What are haemorrhoids?

Symptomatic enlargement and downward displacement of the anal cushions, which are normal aggregations of arterioles, venules, smooth muscle and elastic connective tissue in the submucosa that help seal the anal canal.B&L 80 · p.1430SRB 25 · p.961 They are not varicose veins, which is why you do not excise all of them.

Why is the bleeding bright red?

Because there are direct arteriovenous communications with the submucosal venous dilatations, so the oxygen tension of blood in the anal cushions is more arterial than venous.B&L 80 · p.1419

Why 3, 7 and 11 o'clock?

Because the superior haemorrhoidal vessel divides into two branches on the right and continues as one on the left, giving right anterior, right posterior and left lateral cushions.SRB 25 · p.961 Always state the position of the patient, which is lithotomy, because the clock numbers are meaningless without it.B&L 80 · p.1430

Grade these haemorrhoids.

First degree: bleed only, no prolapse. Second: prolapse and reduce spontaneously. Third: prolapse and need manual reduction. Fourth: permanently prolapsed.B&L 80 · p.1430SRB 25 · p.961

Can you feel internal piles on digital examination?

No. Uncomplicated internal piles cannot be felt with a finger; only chronically inflamed or thrombosed piles can. They are diagnosed on proctoscopy.Das 36 · p.544SRB 25 · p.962

A 55-year-old man with bright rectal bleeding and piles on proctoscopy. What else?

Colonoscopy. Exclusion of other causes of rectal bleeding, especially colorectal malignancy, is the first priority, and the presence of piles does not prove they are the source of the bleeding.B&L 80 · p.1431

Where do you inject a sclerosant, and what goes wrong if you miss?

3 to 5 mL of 5% phenol in almond or arachis oil into the submucosal plane at the apex of the pedicle, above the anorectal ring, not into the haemorrhoid itself.B&L 80 · p.1431 Pain on injection means the needle is too low and should be withdrawn. Too superficial gives rapid white bulging of the mucosa and superficial ulceration. Too deep can cause prostatitis, pelvic sepsis, impotence or a rectovaginal fistula.B&L 80 · p.1431SRB 25 · p.964

Where must a band be placed, and why?

Above the dentate line, about 2 cm above it, on the base of the pedicle. Below the dentate line the mucosa has somatic innervation and a band there causes intense pain.B&L 80 · p.1431SRB 25 · p.964 Warn the patient about bleeding when the pile sloughs at one to two weeks.

Which operation, and what must you not do during it?

Open haemorrhoidectomy, the Milligan-Morgan operation, with the wounds left to heal by secondary intention; or the closed Ferguson technique with the wounds sutured.B&L 80 · p.1432 What you must not do is excise circumferentially: mucocutaneous bridges must be left between the excised pedicles, or the patient gets an anal stenosis.

A patient with strangulated thrombosed prolapsed piles. What do you do?

Most surgeons treat conservatively: adequate analgesia, bed rest, cold saline compresses and laxatives, with resolution usually in three to four days, plus systemic antibiotics to reduce the risk of portal pyaemia. Urgent haemorrhoidectomy may speed symptom resolution but risks later anal stenosis.B&L 80 · p.1430

Why antibiotics in thrombosed piles?

Portal pyaemia. The superior rectal veins drain into the portal system, which is also why portal hypertension is a rare cause of haemorrhoids.B&L 80 · p.1430SRB 25 · p.962

Define an anal fissure.

A longitudinal ulcer in the anoderm of the distal anal canal, extending from the anal verge proximally towards but not beyond the dentate line.B&L 80 · p.1427

Describe a chronic fissure.

A sentinel tag externally, a hypertrophied anal papilla internally, and between them an indurated ulcer whose floor exposes the fibres of the internal sphincter. The tag and the papilla are both consequences of repeated healing and breakdown.B&L 80 · p.1428SRB 25 · p.968

Explain the vicious cycle in a fissure.

Hard stool tears the anoderm; the tear causes pain; pain causes internal sphincter hypertonia; hypertonia reduces blood supply to the anal mucosa, causing relative ischaemia; ischaemia prevents healing, and the repeated trauma deepens the fissure.B&L 80 · p.1428 Every treatment breaks one link in that chain.

A lateral anal fissure. What now?

It is not a simple fissure. A fissure that is not midline, or has atypical features, should raise the suspicion of a specific aetiology, and needs examination under anaesthesia with biopsy and culture to exclude Crohn's disease, tuberculosis, syphilis, Chlamydia, chancroid, lymphogranuloma venereum, herpes, cytomegalovirus, Kaposi's sarcoma, lymphoma or a squamous carcinoma.B&L 80 · p.1428

Medical treatment of a fissure, with doses.

Normalise bowel habit with fibre, stool softeners and water; warm baths and topical local anaesthetic for pain. Then chemical sphincterotomy: GTN 0.2% two or three times daily, or diltiazem 2% twice daily, or botulinum toxin 10 to 100 units into the internal sphincter, which SRB gives as 25 units. Cure rate around 50%. GTN causes headache, which limits compliance; diltiazem and botulinum have similar efficacy with fewer side effects. Botulinum causes temporary incontinence in up to 10%.B&L 80 · p.1428SRB 25 · p.969

Why is the sphincterotomy lateral and not in the floor of the fissure?

Because dividing the internal sphincter in the posterior midline heals as a keyhole deformity, a gutter through which mucus and liquid stool leak. A lateral division achieves the same reduction in sphincter tone in healthy tissue that closes properly, and the fissure then heals on its own, which is why Bailey and Love says the fissure itself needs no specific attention.B&L 80 · p.1428

What are the risks of a lateral internal sphincterotomy?

Healing around 85%, but altered continence in a significant minority: about 9% flatus incontinence, 6% soiling, under 1% incontinence to solid stool. Early complications are haemorrhage, haematoma, bruising, perianal abscess and fistula.B&L 80 · p.1429

A postpartum woman with a chronic anterior fissure. Would you sphincterotomise her?

No. She is at increased risk of altered continence, because her sphincter may already be damaged by delivery and her resting anal pressure may be normal or low, so the fissure is not from hypertonia. Bailey and Love recommends a fissurectomy with an anal advancement flap in exactly this patient: an inverted house-shaped flap of perianal skin mobilised on its blood supply and advanced without tension to cover the fissure.B&L 80 · p.1429

Would you do a Lord's anal dilatation?

No. It is now rarely indicated, because an uncontrolled stretch disrupts the anal sphincters at multiple sites with a real risk of incontinence, and Bailey and Love lists anal dilatation among the causes of faecal incontinence.B&L 80 · pp.1424, 1428 SRB still describes it, manual dilatation under general anaesthesia using four fingers of each hand, Lord 1969, and Recamier's two-fingers-each-hand stretch from 1829.SRB 25 · p.968 The controlled alternatives are chemical sphincterotomy and, if that fails, a lateral internal sphincterotomy.

Define a fistula-in-ano, and give its commonest cause.

A chronic abnormal communication from the anorectal lumen, the internal opening, to an external opening on the perineal or buttock skin, or rarely the vagina. Most are idiopathic or cryptoglandular, arising from infection of an anal gland in the intersphincteric space through its crypt at the dentate line.B&L 80 · p.1436SRB 25 · pp.969, 972

Why is the internal opening at the dentate line?

Because the fistula begins in an anal gland, whose duct opens into a crypt at the dentate line. That is also why curetting the infected gland is an essential part of the operation.SRB 25 · pp.972, 975

Classify anal fistulae.

Park's classification, by the relation of the primary track to the external sphincter: intersphincteric, trans-sphincteric, suprasphincteric and extrasphincteric.B&L 80 · p.1436 The first two account for the great majority. SRB also gives the standard classification, subcutaneous, submucous, low anal, high anal and pelvirectal, and the practical division into low level, opening below the internal ring, and high level, at or above it.SRB 25 · p.972

What is Goodsall's rule, and how far do you trust it?

An anterior external opening usually connects by a straight radial track to an internal opening at the nearest point of the dentate line; a posterior one usually curves to the posterior midline, which is why posterior fistulae may be horseshoe-shaped with several external openings sharing one internal opening. Bailey and Love calls it helpful but notes that the majority of internal openings are midline in both the anterior and posterior planes, so confirm the internal opening with hydrogen peroxide or methylene blue, gentle probing under anaesthesia, and MRI if in doubt.B&L 80 · p.1437SRB 25 · p.973

Which imaging, and why?

MRI is the gold standard, with STIR fat-suppression sequences that show pus and granulation tissue without contrast. Its great advantage is demonstrating secondary extensions, which are missed at surgery and cause persistence. Endoanal ultrasound, especially with hydrogen peroxide, assesses sphincter integrity and whether the fistula is simple or complex. Fistulography and CT are for a suspected extrasphincteric fistula.B&L 80 · p.1438

What is the aim of fistula surgery, and what is the limit?

To keep the patient continent and comfortable and, whenever possible, to eradicate the fistula, in that order. The determinant of function after fistulotomy is the amount of muscle left behind rather than the amount divided, and in a patient with normal bowel habit continence is usually maintained as long as about 2 cm of external sphincter is retained.B&L 80 · p.1439

How does a seton work?

It encircles the muscle superficial to the track and, left tied in place, causes slow ischaemic necrosis, dividing the muscle gradually so the cut ends scar together instead of springing apart, avoiding a gutter deformity and preserving sphincter function. It is a staged fistulotomy, can be kept for about three months, and can be replaced by a railroad technique without anaesthesia.SRB 25 · p.975

Name the sphincter-sparing procedures for a complex fistula.

Seton, as a staged fistulotomy; mucosal advancement flap; fibrin glue, around 70% success; the Surgisis anal fistula plug of porcine small intestinal submucosa, around 85% in simple fistulae; LIFT, ligation of the intersphincteric fistula track; VAAFT, video-assisted anal fistula treatment; and an over-the-scope nitinol fistula clip.SRB 25 · p.975 All of them aim to close the internal opening and obliterate the track without dividing muscle.

Multiple fistula openings with watery discharge and no induration. What do you suspect?

Tuberculosis. SRB: tuberculous fistulae lack induration, have pale granulation tissue and watery discharge, and are most often multiple.SRB 25 · p.974 Also consider Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, lymphogranuloma venereum, hidradenitis suppurativa, actinomycosis and malignancy, so biopsy before definitive treatment.SRB 25 · pp.972, 974

What causes a suprasphincteric fistula?

Often the surgeon. Bailey and Love: they are often caused by excessive probing of an abscess cavity or fistula tract during examination under anaesthesia.B&L 80 · p.1437 Which is why probing is done gently, under anaesthesia, and never in the ward.

Why might a fistula recur after apparently adequate surgery?

A missed secondary extension. That is Bailey and Love's stated reason for preferring MRI, whose great advantage is demonstrating extensions that are missed at surgery and cause persistence.B&L 80 · p.1438 Other reasons are an unrecognised specific cause such as Crohn's or tuberculosis, and failure to curette the infected anal gland.
