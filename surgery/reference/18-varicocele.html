Short case 18 · General Surgery

Varicocele

Nine out of ten are on the left, and the reason is pure anatomy. The examiner will want that reason, the fertility figures, and the one circumstance in which a varicocele is a sign of something far worse.

S Das: clinical method Bailey & Love: mechanism and management SRB: exam framework

1Definition, incidence and the left-sided question

Definition
B&L: "A varicocele is an abnormal dilatation and enlargement of the scrotal venous plexus draining the testis."B&L 86 · p.1563
Incidence, with all four figures
"Varicoceles are common, affecting 10–20% of adult males. About 90% are left sided." "If a left varicocele is identified, there is a 30–40% probability that it is a bilateral condition." "They are unusual in boys and typically develop during late childhood and adolescence." And the figure that matters most: "Varicoceles occur in around 15–20% of all males but are found in about 40% of infertile males."B&L 86 · p.1563
SoCommon in the general population, twice as common in infertile men, and if you find one on the left there is a one in three chance the right is involved too.
Why the left, which is the examiner's favourite
B&L gives the anatomical asymmetry: the right testicular vein drains "into the low-pressure inferior vena cava below the right renal vein",B&L 86 · p.1563 whereas the left drains into the left renal vein. Three consequences follow, and a complete answer gives all three:
1. The left vein is longer, so the column of blood it must support is taller.
2. It joins the left renal vein at a right angle, rather than obliquely into the cava.
3. It drains into a higher-pressure vein, the renal vein, rather than directly into the low-pressure cava.
The anatomy of the plexus
B&L: "The veins draining the testis and the epididymis form the pampiniform plexus. The veins gradually join each other as they traverse the inguinal canal."B&L 86 · p.1563 So the varicosity you feel in the scrotum is the pampiniform plexus, and the operation is performed where those veins have coalesced.
The one presentation that is an emergency of a different kind A varicocele of sudden onset, on the right, or one that does not decompress when the patient lies down, raises an abdominal or retroperitoneal mass obstructing the testicular vein, classically a renal cell carcinoma invading the renal vein on the left. The reasoning comes straight from the drainage anatomy B&L gives: the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein, so a tumour in the renal vein obstructs it.B&L 86 · p.1563

So the two questions that convert a trivial case into a serious one are: which side, and does it empty on lying flat? A right-sided or non-decompressing varicocele needs imaging of the abdomen, not a scrotal operation.

2Examination

Examine standing, then lying
Like any venous lesion, a varicocele fills against gravity, so it is seen and felt standing and should decompress on lying down. B&L's illustration is of a large varicocele in a pendulous scrotum, with a coexisting inguinal hernia.B&L 86 · p.1563
The classical description
A soft, irregular swelling in the upper scrotum behind and above the testis, which has been likened to a bag of worms. It is separate from the testis, which should be palpable normally below it.
Cough impulse
Present, and this matters because it is a shared false positive with hernia. B&L's hernia chapter lists it: a varicocele, like a saphena varix, gives a cough impulse and is referred as a suspected hernia.B&L 64 · p.1061 S Das makes the same point: a cough impulse does not prove a hernia.Das 38 · p.599
Measure the testis, both sides
Because testicular atrophy on the affected side is one of the accepted indications for repair in an adolescent, and because it is objective evidence of harm.
And examine the abdomen
For a mass, and for a kidney. Particularly if the varicocele is right-sided, of recent onset, or fails to decompress.
Grading, for completeness The conventional clinical grading is: subclinical, detectable only on Doppler; grade 1, palpable only on Valsalva; grade 2, palpable without Valsalva; grade 3, visible through the scrotal skin. It is worth offering, because it is how series and trials describe their patients, and B&L illustrates the Doppler appearance of a left varicocele alongside the clinical photograph.B&L 86 · p.1563

3Why it matters: the fertility question

The association, in B&L's figures
Varicoceles occur in around 15 to 20% of all males but are found in about 40% of infertile males.B&L 86 · p.1563 That is an association, and the direction of causation is what the examiner is probing.
The proposed mechanisms
Raised scrotal temperature from the pooled venous blood, impairing spermatogenesis; venous stasis with hypoxia; and reflux of adrenal metabolites down the left testicular vein from the renal vein. Testicular atrophy on the affected side is the visible end point.
And the honest position
The association is strong, the mechanism is plausible, and the evidence that repair improves live birth rates is weaker than the evidence that it improves semen parameters. So the defensible answer is: repair for symptoms, for testicular atrophy in an adolescent, and in a man being investigated for infertility with abnormal semen parameters and a palpable varicocele, rather than for every varicocele that is found.

4Investigations

Scrotal ultrasound with Doppler
Confirms the dilated plexus and demonstrates reflux; B&L shows the Doppler ultrasound of a left varicocele.B&L 86 · p.1563 It also measures testicular volume, which is the objective evidence of harm.
Semen analysis
Where fertility is the issue, before and after any intervention. The numbers that justify operating are the semen parameters, not the size of the varicocele.
Imaging of the abdomen
For a right-sided varicocele, a sudden-onset varicocele, or one that does not decompress on lying down: ultrasound or CT of the abdomen to exclude a retroperitoneal or renal mass obstructing the testicular vein. The anatomy that makes this necessary is the drainage of the left testicular vein into the left renal vein.B&L 86 · p.1563

5Treatment

When to leave it alone
Most varicoceles. They affect 10 to 20% of all adult men and the majority cause neither symptoms nor infertility.B&L 86 · p.1563 Reassurance and scrotal support are enough.
When to repair
Pain or a dragging sensation not relieved by support; testicular atrophy, particularly in an adolescent; and infertility with abnormal semen parameters and a palpable varicocele.
How, and what the evidence says
B&L compares the approaches and comes down on the side of the newer ones: there are varicocelectomy techniques "with fewer complications and lower recurrence rates".B&L 86 · p.1564 The options are:
Microsurgical subinguinal or inguinal varicocelectomy, which allows the veins to be ligated individually while preserving the testicular artery and lymphatics, and which is generally regarded as having the lowest recurrence and hydrocele rate.
Laparoscopic high ligation of the testicular veins.
Open retroperitoneal high ligation, the Palomo approach.
Radiological embolisation of the testicular vein.
The complications to name
Recurrence, from missed collateral veins; hydrocele, from ligation of the lymphatics, which is the commonest complication of non-microsurgical techniques; and testicular atrophy, from injury to the testicular artery. Preserving the artery and the lymphatics is what the microsurgical technique is for.B&L 86 · p.1564

6Rapid-fire viva

Define a varicocele and give its incidence.

An abnormal dilatation and enlargement of the scrotal venous plexus draining the testis. It affects 10 to 20% of adult males, about 90% are left sided, and if a left varicocele is found there is a 30 to 40% probability the condition is bilateral. They are unusual in boys and typically develop in late childhood and adolescence.B&L 86 · p.1563

Why are 90% left sided?

Because of the drainage asymmetry. The right testicular vein drains into the low-pressure inferior vena cava below the right renal vein, whereas the left drains into the left renal vein.B&L 86 · p.1563 So on the left the vein is longer, supporting a taller column of blood; it joins at a right angle rather than obliquely; and it drains into a higher-pressure vein rather than directly into the cava.

What is the relationship with infertility?

Varicoceles occur in around 15 to 20% of all males but are found in about 40% of infertile males.B&L 86 · p.1563 That is a strong association; the proposed mechanisms are raised scrotal temperature from pooled venous blood impairing spermatogenesis, venous stasis with hypoxia, and reflux of adrenal metabolites down the left testicular vein. But the evidence that repair improves live birth rates is weaker than the evidence that it improves semen parameters, so I would repair for symptoms, for testicular atrophy in an adolescent, or for infertility with abnormal semen parameters and a palpable varicocele, rather than for every varicocele found.

A right-sided varicocele of recent onset. What are you worried about?

An abdominal or retroperitoneal mass obstructing the testicular vein. The anatomy makes this logical: the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein and the right into the cava,B&L 86 · p.1563 so a new varicocele on the unusual side, or one that fails to decompress on lying down, suggests obstruction rather than simple valvular incompetence, classically renal cell carcinoma invading the renal vein. Image the abdomen rather than operating on the scrotum.

Which veins are involved?

The pampiniform plexus, formed by the veins draining the testis and epididymis, which gradually join each other as they traverse the inguinal canal.B&L 86 · p.1563 That coalescence is why a high ligation can control the varicocele with few vessels, and why missed collaterals cause recurrence.

A groin and scrotal swelling with a cough impulse. Is it a hernia?

Not necessarily. A varicocele gives a cough impulse, as does a saphena varix, and both are referred as suspected inguinal hernias.B&L 64 · p.1061Das 38 · p.599 Look for an expansile impulse, for reducibility, and for whether you can get above the swelling: you can get above a varicocele and feel normal cord above it, and it decompresses on lying down.

How would you grade a varicocele?

Subclinical, detectable only on Doppler; grade 1, palpable only on Valsalva; grade 2, palpable without Valsalva; grade 3, visible through the scrotal skin. Doppler ultrasound confirms the dilated plexus, demonstrates reflux and measures testicular volume.B&L 86 · p.1563

When would you operate?

For pain or a dragging sensation not relieved by scrotal support; for testicular atrophy, particularly in an adolescent; and for infertility with abnormal semen parameters and a palpable varicocele. Not for an incidental asymptomatic varicocele with normal semen parameters, since 10 to 20% of all men have one.B&L 86 · p.1563

What operations are available, and which is preferred?

Microsurgical subinguinal or inguinal varicocelectomy, laparoscopic high ligation, open retroperitoneal high ligation by the Palomo approach, and radiological embolisation of the testicular vein. Bailey and Love notes varicocelectomy techniques with fewer complications and lower recurrence rates,B&L 86 · p.1564 and the microsurgical approach is generally preferred because individual veins can be ligated while preserving the testicular artery and the lymphatics.

What are the complications of varicocele repair?

Recurrence, from missed collateral veins; hydrocele, from ligating the lymphatics, which is the commonest complication of non-microsurgical techniques; and testicular atrophy, from injury to the testicular artery. Preserving the artery and lymphatics is precisely what the microsurgical technique achieves.B&L 86 · p.1564

Why examine the patient standing?

Because a varicocele is a venous lesion that fills against gravity, so it is best seen and felt standing and should decompress on lying down. Bailey and Love's illustration is of a large varicocele in a pendulous scrotum.B&L 86 · p.1563 Failure to decompress on lying flat is the finding that should send you to image the abdomen.
