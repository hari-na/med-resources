Long case 06 · General Surgery

Right Iliac Fossa Mass

A differential diagnosis case before it is anything else. Four conditions account for almost all of them, and the whole examination exists to separate them by three properties: consistency, percussion note, and whether the caecum has been pulled out of position.

S Das: clinical method Bailey & Love: mechanism and management SRB: exam framework

1The case in one breath

The five that matter, in SRB's own order "Common masses are appendicular mass; carcinoma caecum; ileocaecal tuberculosis; lymph node mass; ameboma."SRB 16 · p.723 Learn those five thoroughly and the rest of the list as names. In India the first three carry almost all the cases, and B&L's own differential for a right iliac fossa mass in its tuberculosis section is nearly identical: appendix mass, lymphoma, carcinoma of the caecum, Crohn's disease, tuberculosis or actinomycosis.B&L 74 · p.1308

Is an appendicular mass smooth or irregular? SRB 16 p.723 and SRB 24 p.937 say smooth with distinct borders; S Das 35 p.533 says irregular
SRB, twice
"Appendicular mass: it is smooth, firm, tender mass in the right iliac fossa; it is not mobile; it does not move with respiration; it is resonant on percussion. It is a well-localised mass with distinct borders."SRB 16 · p.723 And in its appendix chapter: "resonant, smooth, firm, and tender with well defined borders which does not move with respiration and does not have mobility".SRB 24 · p.937
S Das
"The lump is irregular, firm, tender, and fixed. It may be tympanitic on percussion, but it is so tender that the patient may not allow proper percussion to be performed."Das 35 · p.533 And he explains why it would be irregular: the mass consists of inflamed appendix, greater omentum, oedematous caecal wall surrounded by coils of small intestine matted together with lymph.Das 35 · p.533
Live disagreement on a finding you will be asked to report out loud, and the honest answer favours S Das, because his reasoning is anatomical: a mass made of appendix plus omentum plus caecal wall plus matted small bowel loops is not a smooth object. Report what you actually feel, and describe the thing rather than reaching for an adjective: "a firm, tender mass in the right iliac fossa, about this size, with a fairly well-defined margin but an uneven surface, not moving with respiration, not mobile, resonant where I can percuss it." That sentence satisfies both books.

Then add the caveat that matters clinically, which is also S Das's: the mass is so tender, and the overlying muscle so rigid, that you may not be able to define it properly at all. He says the tender mass "may not be so easy to feel" beneath the rigidity of the abdominal musculature.Das 35 · p.533 A candidate who says "I could not fully delineate it because of guarding" is more believable than one who confidently reports smooth borders.
Mass or abscess? The distinction is pus, and three signs find it S Das is categorical: "appendicular mass does not contain any pus at all, the appendicular abscess contains pus".Das 35 · p.533 The three things that tell you which:

1. Percussion. The mass is resonant, because it contains bowel; the abscess is dull, because it contains fluid.SRB 16 · p.723
2. Borders. Distinct in a mass, indistinct in an abscess.SRB 16 · p.723
3. The systemic response. Variable pyrexia and a rising leucocyte count with polymorphonuclear leucocytosis are features of the abscess.Das 35 · p.533

And the fourth, late sign: an appendicular abscess tends to approach the surface, so redness and oedema of the abdominal wall become evident.Das 35 · p.533 That matters because it changes the treatment from antibiotics to drainage.

Why an appendicular mass forms at all

It is the body walling off the infection
S Das: the mass is "an attempt by nature to make a protective wall around the inflamed appendix to prevent general peritonitis even if the appendix perforates".Das 35 · p.533 That sentence is the entire rationale for treating it conservatively, so learn it: if nature has already localised the infection, breaking into it undoes the work.
When it appears
Gradually, on the third day or earlier after the onset of acute appendicitis, and the mass invariably coincides with the position of the appendix.Das 35 · p.533 So a retrocaecal appendix gives a mass you feel high and laterally, and a pelvic appendix may give nothing abdominal at all and only a rectal finding.
What it is made of
SRB's figure names the components: dilated ileum, greater omentum, inflamed appendix and caecum.SRB 24 · p.937 B&L's word for the same thing is a phlegmonous mass or paracaecal abscess.B&L 76 · p.1338
Who perforates and walls off badly
B&L: extremes of age, immunosuppression, diabetes mellitus, faecolith obstruction of the lumen, a free-lying pelvic appendix, and previous abdominal surgery that limits the greater omentum's ability to wall off the spread.B&L 76 · p.1338 SRB's list is almost identical: children, old age, faecolith, laxative abuse, diabetes mellitus, immunosuppression and a pelvic appendix.SRB 24 · p.936 Note that the last of the omentum's jobs is the one that explains why previous surgery is a risk factor, and it is the detail candidates never give.

3History, and why each question earns its place

How long has the lump been there? Days, weeks, or months? DistinguishesThis single question splits the differential in two. Days, after an attack of abdominal pain: appendicular mass or abscess, since the mass develops on the third day or earlier.Das 35 · p.533 Weeks to months, with ill health: tuberculosis, carcinoma, Crohn's or lymphoma.
SoAsk it first, and say out loud which half of the list you are now in. Examiners reward a candidate who narrows before he elaborates.
Tell me about the pain that started it. Where did it begin and where did it go? Rules inClassical appendicitis: pain beginning centrally and shifting to the right iliac fossa, with anorexia, nausea and vomiting, then localised tenderness. An appendicular mass is the sequel to that story, not a separate illness.
Rules in something elsePain over two places, the caecum and the sigmoid colon, with diarrhoea: S Das's description of amoebic typhlitis, which he says "hardly produces a lump".Das 35 · p.534 So pain in two places argues against a single localised mass.
CarefulS Das's warning about amoebiasis in tropical practice: "One must be very careful to exclude this condition before one does an appendicectomy."Das 35 · p.534 That is an Indian caution the British text does not make.
Any fever? At what time of day? DistinguishesEvening rise of temperature with a right iliac fossa lump and ill health "should arouse the suspicion of" hyperplastic ileocaecal tuberculosis.Das 35 · p.533 Continuous fever with rigors and a tender dull mass suggests an abscess. Absence of fever with a hard nodular mass in an elderly patient suggests carcinoma.
And in management it is a monitorIn the conservative regime B&L requires temperature and pulse recorded 4-hourly, with a rising pulse rate as the first criterion to abandon it.B&L 76 · p.1343
Any change in bowel habit? Diarrhoea, or alternating with constipation? Any blood? Rules inRecurrent abdominal pain with diarrhoea and the features of blind loop syndrome, meaning anaemia, loss of weight and steatorrhoea, is S Das's history for hyperplastic ileocaecal tuberculosis.Das 35 · p.533 B&L gives the same mechanism: incomplete ileal obstruction leads to stasis and bacterial overgrowth, which in turn causes steatorrhoea, anaemia and loss of weight.B&L 74 · p.1308 Learn that chain; it earns marks because it explains three symptoms from one lesion.
Rules inOccult blood in the stool with anaemia and rapid loss of weight: carcinoma of the caecum.Das 35 · p.533 A right-sided colonic cancer bleeds occultly and presents with anaemia rather than with obstruction, because the caecum is capacious and the stool there is liquid.
And a history of dysenteryPoints to amoeboma.SRB 16 · p.723
Any perianal problem? Pain, discharge, a fissure or fistula? Rules inCrohn's disease. S Das's discriminator: "Anal complications such as fissure and multiple fistulae are mainly seen in Crohn's disease. Fistulae may even be seen in the right iliac fossa in Crohn's disease."Das 35 · p.533
Rules outB&L's contrast for tuberculosis: "Unlike in Crohn's disease, abscess and fistula formation are uncommon."B&L 74 · p.1308 So a fistula points to Crohn's and away from tuberculosis, which is a distinction that is otherwise very hard to make, since the two look alike radiologically and histologically.
SoExamine the perineum. It is one question and one look, and it can settle the case.
Any cough, night sweats, contact with tuberculosis, or previous treatment for it? Rules inTuberculosis, and it matters which type. B&L separates two: ulcerative tuberculosis "develops secondary to pulmonary tuberculosis and arises as a result of swallowing tubercle bacilli", so there is active lung disease; and hyperplastic tuberculosis, which is "caused by the ingestion of Mycobacterium tuberculosis by patients with a high resistance to the organism".B&L 74 · p.1308
SoThe patient with a mass is usually the hyperplastic type, and by definition relatively resistant, so the chest may be clear. A normal chest radiograph does not exclude abdominal tuberculosis, and saying that shows you understand the mechanism rather than the association.
Which presents howUlcerative: diarrhoea and weight loss, sometimes subacute obstruction, local perforation or fistula. Hyperplastic: attacks of abdominal pain and intermittent diarrhoea, and "patients may present with a mass in the right iliac fossa and vague ill health".B&L 74 · p.1308
Any vomiting, distension or absolute constipation? Rules inObstruction, which changes the plan. SRB on carcinoma caecum: "often features of intestinal obstruction may be present".SRB 16 · p.723 For tuberculosis it is typically incomplete or subacute obstruction.B&L 74 · p.1308
And it decides the operationIn tuberculosis, B&L's rule is explicit: medical treatment when the diagnosis is clear and the patient has not yet developed obstructive symptoms; ileocaecal resection where obstruction is present.B&L 74 · p.1308 So this question chooses between drugs and an operation.
Age, and in a female, the menstrual and obstetric history. Is there any chance of pregnancy? WhyBecause a third of SRB's differential is gynaecological: ovarian cyst, tubo-ovarian mass, pedunculated fibroid.SRB 16 · p.723 And B&L lists a pregnancy test among the selective preoperative investigations in suspected appendicitis.B&L 76 · p.1342
And age shifts the whole listCarcinoma of the caecum is a disease of the elderly.Das 35 · p.533 Appendicular mass is a young person's lesion. Tuberculosis spans both.
ButAge never excludes: B&L notes that obstruction of the appendiceal orifice by tumour, particularly carcinoma of the caecum, is an occasional cause of acute appendicitis in middle-aged and elderly patients.B&L 76 · p.1337 So in an older patient the appendicitis itself may be a symptom of the cancer.

4Examination, and what each finding settles

First: is the swelling in the wall or in the abdomen?

Ask the supine patient to lift both legs off the bed with the knees extended, or to raise the head and shoulders with the arms folded across the chest. WhyThis is the leg-lifting test, S Das's Carnett's manoeuvre, performed "to know whether the swelling is parietal or intra-abdominal".Das 35 · p.520
Rules inSRB's interpretation: contracting the abdominal muscles makes an intra-abdominal mass less prominent, while a parietal mass persists the same but becomes less mobile.SRB 16 · p.723
And one more parietal clueS Das: "Does the swelling pit on pressure? Pitting of the skin is the feature of a parietal abscess." Pitting can also be demonstrated in a colon loaded with faeces.Das 35 · p.519
SoDo this before anything else, because if the mass is parietal the entire intra-abdominal differential is irrelevant, and most candidates never test it.

Then palpate the mass properly

Surface, consistency, borders
See the table above, and report what you feel rather than what the book says you should feel. Remember the two generalisations: hard commonly means malignant,SRB 16 · p.724 and soft with indistinct borders and dullness means pus.SRB 16 · p.723
Does it move with respiration?
None of the five common right iliac fossa masses does.SRB 16 · p.723 So if it does move with respiration, you are feeling liver, gallbladder, or a kidney, and your differential is wrong.
Mobility, and what restricts it
Carcinoma caecum is mobile, because the caecum is on a mesentery, but mobility may be restricted once it becomes adherent to psoas. Ileocaecal tuberculosis has restricted mobility. Appendicular mass and abscess and amoeboma are not mobile.SRB 16 · p.723 So paradoxically the cancer is the mobile mass, which is the opposite of what students expect.
Percussion, which separates mass from abscess
Resonant: appendicular mass, ileocaecal tuberculosis, and carcinoma caecum (resonant or impaired). Dull: appendicular abscess.SRB 16 · p.723 S Das's honest caveat: the mass "is so tender that the patient may not allow proper percussion to be performed".Das 35 · p.533
The sign that clinches tuberculosis: where is the caecum? In ileocaecal tuberculosis the caecum is pulled up out of the right iliac fossa. SRB: "caecum may be pulled up to lumbar region due to fibrosis".SRB 16 · p.723 S Das uses the same sign as his key differential from Crohn's: "In Crohn's disease the caecum remains in its position and is not elevated as in this condition",Das 35 · p.533 and on barium a caecum in a high up position is part of the tuberculous picture.Das 35 · p.534

So the examinable chain is: tuberculosis fibroses and shortens, which pulls the caecum up; Crohn's inflames and thickens, which leaves it where it is.

Then the rest of the patient

The psoas sign, for a psoas abscess
SRB: a psoas abscess is localised, smooth, soft and non-mobile, and psoas spasm, meaning flexion of the hip joint, is typical. Examine the spine: gibbus, tenderness, paraspinal spasm, and restricted spinal movements.SRB 16 · p.723 A right iliac fossa mass with a flexed hip and a tender spine is spinal tuberculosis with a cold abscess tracking down the psoas sheath, and it needs no laparotomy at all.
Rectal and vaginal examination: not optional
SRB: "In all lower abdomen masses per rectal and/or per vaginal examination is a must."SRB 16 · p.723 It finds a pelvic abscess, a pelvic appendix, an ovarian mass and a rectovesical deposit, all of which are invisible from above.
Lymph nodes and the liver
For lymphoma, and for secondaries from a caecal carcinoma. A hard nodular liver with a right iliac fossa mass settles the diagnosis and the prognosis in one examination.
And look for ascites
Peritoneal tuberculosis and peritoneal carcinomatosis both cause it. SRB describes the puddle sign in the knee-elbow position for minimal ascites,SRB 16 · p.723 and the Blaxland ruler test: a flat ruler pressed firmly backwards on the lower abdomen just above the anterosuperior iliac spines transmits aortic pulsation to the fingers in an ovarian cyst but not in ascites.SRB 16 · p.723

5Investigations, and why each one

Blood: full count, and what the differential tells you
Leucocytosis with polymorphonuclear predominance supports an abscess.Das 35 · p.533 SRB: in an appendicular mass the total count is increased.SRB 24 · p.937 Microcytic anaemia with a hard mass in an older patient points to carcinoma caecum, which bleeds occultly.Das 35 · p.533 B&L's routine preoperative tests in suspected appendicitis are a full blood count and urinalysis, with pregnancy test, urea and electrolytes, C-reactive protein as selective.B&L 76 · p.1342
Stool examination
For occult blood, which supports carcinoma, and for amoebae, which supports amoeboma or amoebic typhlitis. SRB lists stool and urine examination among the baseline investigations for any abdominal mass.SRB 16 · p.723
Ultrasound: first imaging
SRB: ultrasound confirms the mass in suspected appendicular mass,SRB 24 · p.937 and ultrasound of the abdomen and pelvis is on B&L's selective list.B&L 76 · p.1342 Its real value is distinguishing a solid mass from a collection, which decides whether something needs draining.
Contrast-enhanced CT: the modern standard here
B&L makes it part of the conservative regime itself: "A contrast-enhanced CT examination of the abdomen should be performed and antibiotic therapy instigated."B&L 76 · p.1343 In tuberculosis CT "will also demonstrate the narrowed segment with proximal distension and the associated lymphadenopathy".B&L 74 · p.1308 It answers three questions at once: is there a collection to drain, is there a tumour, and are there nodes.
Colonoscopy
Once the acute episode has settled, to exclude carcinoma and to biopsy a tuberculous or Crohn's lesion. SRB lists colonoscopy among the investigations for an abdominal mass.SRB 16 · p.723 And in amoebic typhlitis S Das says the sigmoidoscope is of great help in diagnosis.Das 35 · p.534
Image-guided biopsy
Ultrasound-guided or CT-guided biopsy is on SRB's list,SRB 16 · p.723 and it is how a lymphoma or a tuberculous node is diagnosed without a laparotomy.
And when all else fails
Diagnostic laparoscopy and then exploratory laparotomy.SRB 16 · p.723 S Das's defence of it still stands in Indian practice: where investigation is limited, laparotomy "becomes the last court of appeal".Das 34 · p.507
The barium findings that separate the three chronic causes S Das's three-way radiological discrimination, which is one of the most answerable questions in this case.Das 35 · p.534

Hyperplastic ileocaecal tuberculosis: a long narrow constricted terminal ileum and ascending colon, with the caecum high up. B&L agrees: "a barium follow-through or small bowel enema will show a long narrow filling defect in the terminal ileum", adding candidly that this "may result in a differential diagnosis of Crohn's disease".B&L 74 · p.1308
Crohn's disease: a narrow and smooth terminal ileum, the string sign of Kantor, that segment devoid of peristaltic waves, and the caecum normal in the majority.
Carcinoma of the caecum: an irregular filling defect affecting the caecum with a soft tissue shadow showing the extent of the tumour, but the terminal ileum absolutely normal.

So: both TB and Crohn's narrow the ileum, and the caecum tells them apart; the cancer spares the ileum entirely.

6Treatment, and the reasoning behind each choice

The appendicular mass: the Ochsner-Sherren regime

Why conservative, in B&L's own words "If an appendix mass is present and the condition of the patient is satisfactory, the standard treatment is the conservative Ochsner-Sherren regime. This strategy is based on the premise that the inflammatory process is already localised and that inadvertent surgery is difficult and may be dangerous. It may be impossible to find the appendix and, occasionally, a faecal fistula may form."B&L 76 · p.1343 SRB puts the same reasoning more briefly: "as nature has already localised the infection, if now disturbed will cause faecal fistula".SRB 24 · p.937

Two reasons, then: the appendix may be unfindable, and the complication is a faecal fistula. Give both.
What the regime actually consists of
SRB's list: observation with temperature, blood pressure and pulse charting; marking the mass to identify progression or regression; antibiotics, naming ampicillin, metronidazole and gentamicin by severity and requirement; intravenous fluids; analgesics; and initial nasogastric aspiration.SRB 24 · p.937 B&L adds the specifics: mark the limits of the mass on the abdominal wall with a skin pencil; record temperature and pulse 4-hourly; maintain a fluid balance record; perform a contrast-enhanced CT; instigate antibiotics, often metronidazole and a third-generation cephalosporin; and drain an abscess radiologically if one is present.B&L 76 · p.1343
What success looks like
B&L: "Clinical improvement is usually evident within 24 to 48 hours", and using this regime "approximately 90% of cases resolve without incident".B&L 76 · p.1343 SRB: the patient usually responds by 48 to 72 hours, the mass reduces in size, and temperature and pulse settle.SRB 24 · p.937 S Das: with conservative treatment the mass gradually becomes smaller and disappears, which can be verified by outlining the mass with a skin pencil.Das 35 · p.533 All three agree, and all three name the skin pencil.
And the rule for failure
B&L: "Clinical deterioration or evidence of peritonitis is an indication for early laparotomy", and "failure of the mass to resolve should raise suspicion of a carcinoma or Crohn's disease".B&L 76 · p.1343 Those are two different failures with two different meanings: getting worse means operate, failing to resolve means re-think the diagnosis.
How many criteria for abandoning conservative treatment? B&L 76 p.1343 prints three; SRB 24 p.937 lists five
Bailey & Love 28e, Summary box 76.6
Three: a rising pulse rate; increasing or spreading abdominal pain; increasing size of the mass.B&L 76 · p.1343
SRB
Five: the patient becomes more toxic, with tachycardia and high fever; persistent vomiting; increase or spread of abdominal pain, meaning the onset of diffuse peritonitis; increased size of the mass; and suppuration, that is abscess formation, in the mass.SRB 24 · p.937
Not contradictory, but SRB's list is the safer one to give because it includes the two that actually change what you do. Answer with SRB's five, grouped so they sound like reasoning rather than recitation: "I would stop if the patient became more toxic with tachycardia and fever, if he vomited persistently, if the pain increased or spread suggesting diffuse peritonitis, if the mass enlarged, or if it suppurated into an abscess." B&L's three are the core, and a rising pulse rate is the single most important early sign in all accounts, so name it first.

The reason the two extra items matter: suppuration does not necessarily mean laparotomy, because B&L says an abscess "should be drained radiologically",B&L 76 · p.1343 so detecting it changes you from antibiotics to drainage rather than straight to surgery. And persistent vomiting flags developing obstruction. A candidate who gives three criteria and then cannot say what to do when an abscess forms has learned the box and not the management.
Is interval appendicectomy mandatory? SRB 24 p.936–937 says yes at 6 weeks; B&L 76 p.1343 says it is "much debated" and reframes the reason entirely
SRB
Unconditional: "Appendicular mass is initially treated with Ochsner Sherren regime. After 6 weeks, interval appendicectomy is done."SRB 24 · p.936 And the patient "is discharged and advised to come for interval appendicectomy after 6 weeks".SRB 24 · p.937 For an appendicular abscess, after drainage, SRB's interval is 3 months.SRB 24 · p.938
Bailey & Love 28e
Rejects the traditional reason and supplies a new one. On recurrence: "The need for interval appendicectomy in this cohort is much debated. The majority of patients will not develop recurrent appendicitis." But on malignancy: "recently published studies have identified higher than expected rates of underlying appendiceal neoplasm in those patients who do go on to interval appendicectomy, particularly those patients over the age of 40. A recent randomised clinical trial was terminated early owing to the unexpected finding of appendiceal tumour during follow-up in 29% (12/41) of patients aged more than 40 years who initially presented with periappendicular abscess."B&L 76 · p.1343 And as a general rule: "patients over the age of 40 should be followed up to ensure that there is no underlying malignancy".B&L 76 · p.1343
Live disagreement, and the modern evidence has turned the indication upside down: interval appendicectomy is now justified less to prevent recurrence and more to exclude a tumour. Answer it in that structure, which lets you agree with your examiner and still show you have read the current edition:

"The classical teaching is interval appendicectomy at six weeks after a mass, and three months after a drained abscess, to prevent recurrent appendicitis. Current evidence questions that rationale, because most patients never get recurrent appendicitis. But it strengthens the case for intervention on different grounds: in patients over 40 who presented with a periappendicular abscess, an underlying appendiceal neoplasm is found far more often than expected, in one trial in 29%, enough that the trial was stopped early. So I would certainly investigate or resect in a patient over 40, and I would discuss it rather than mandate it in a young patient."SRB 24 · p.936, p.938B&L 76 · p.1343

Note how this joins up with the examination: B&L's warning that failure of the mass to resolve should raise suspicion of a carcinoma or Crohn's diseaseB&L 76 · p.1343 and its note that caecal carcinoma can obstruct the appendiceal orifice and cause the appendicitis.B&L 76 · p.1337 In an older patient, the appendicitis may be the presenting symptom of the cancer.
Is conservative treatment contraindicated in children? SRB 24 p.937 lists children and the elderly as contraindications; B&L 76 p.1343 reports it is safe in children
SRB's contraindications to the Ochsner-Sherren regimen
Four: "1. When diagnosis is in doubt. 2. In acute appendicitis in children and elderly. 3. In burst, gangrenous appendicitis. 4. In patients in whom diffuse peritonitis sets in."SRB 24 · p.937 Its reason, given elsewhere, is that early peritonitis is the danger and that children and the elderly are among the high-risk groups for perforation.SRB 24 · p.936
Bailey & Love 28e
Reports the opposite from trial data: "In children, conservative treatment of uncomplicated appendicitis also appears to be safe in the short term with resolution of acute symptoms in approximately 90% of patients. Re-presentation with complicated appendicitis appears to be very rare; however, recurrent symptoms requiring surgery have been reported in up to 46% of patients. Notably, histological features of acute appendicitis may be present in only one-fifth of patients needing interval surgery."B&L 76 · p.1343
These are answers to two different questions, and spotting that is the answer. SRB is talking about acute appendicitis in a child, where the risk of rapid perforation argues for prompt appendicectomy. B&L is talking about conservative antibiotic treatment of uncomplicated appendicitis, a newer strategy, and reporting that it is short-term safe but fails often, with up to 46% needing surgery anyway.

So answer: "For an established appendicular mass, the Ochsner-Sherren regime is standard at any age, with the usual stopping criteria. For acute appendicitis in a child I would operate rather than treat with antibiotics, because the perforation risk is high and the failure rate of antibiotic treatment is up to 46%. SRB's contraindications are about acute appendicitis, not about a mass that has already walled off."SRB 24 · p.937B&L 76 · p.1343

Keep SRB's other three contraindications, because they are sound and uncontested: doubt about the diagnosis, a burst or gangrenous appendix, and established diffuse peritonitis. The first is the most important: you cannot watch a mass you have not diagnosed.
Only in one book: antibiotics instead of appendicectomy B&L 76 p.1342–1343 gives the trial evidence; neither Indian text teaches it as an option
Bailey & Love 28e
"While surgery remains the standard teaching, there is evidence to support a trial of conservative management in patients"B&L 76 · p.1342 with uncomplicated appendicitis, defined as absence of appendicolith, perforation or abscess. Treatment is bowel rest and intravenous antibiotics, often metronidazole and a third-generation cephalosporin. The data: initial success in approximately 85%; but between one-quarter and one-third of patients will require surgery within one year for recurrent symptoms. Reassuringly, subsequent surgery tends to be uncomplicated, the overall postoperative complication rate is similar, and hospital length of stay is also similar. Its verdict: "antibiotic treatment of acute uncomplicated appendicitis appears to be safe and it allows a large number of patients to avoid invasive treatment; however, this information must be balanced by the high treatment failure rate and need for interval intervention", and it should be considered in the well patient with limited signs or those with high operative risk, who "must be aware of the high failure rate".B&L 76 · p.1343
S Das and SRB
Neither offers antibiotics as definitive treatment for acute appendicitis. SRB's conservative regime is for an established mass, and it explicitly contraindicates conservative management when the appendix has burst or peritonitis has set in.SRB 24 · p.937
This is an addition rather than a disagreement, so present it as one and do not lead with it. In an Indian exam, appendicectomy remains the answer for acute appendicitis, and saying otherwise unprompted risks sounding as though you are avoiding the operation. But if asked whether appendicitis can ever be treated without surgery, the current answer is yes, with conditions, and it is worth stating precisely: "In uncomplicated appendicitis, meaning no appendicolith, no perforation and no abscess, intravenous antibiotics succeed initially in about 85%, but a quarter to a third need surgery within a year, so it is reserved for the well patient with limited signs or the patient at high operative risk, after counselling about the failure rate."B&L 76 · p.1343 The one group where the evidence is weakest is children, where recurrence has been reported up to 46%.

The appendicular abscess

Drain it, and preferably radiologically
B&L: "An abscess, if present, should be drained radiologically."B&L 76 · p.1343 This is the modern first choice and it avoids a laparotomy in an inflamed field.
If it is drained surgically
SRB: a drain is placed through a separate incision, antibiotics are continued, and interval appendicectomy is done after 3 months.SRB 24 · p.938
Where these abscesses form, which is a classic list
SRB names the sites: retrocaecal, appendicular, pelvic, subphrenic, lumbar and preileal, and also portal pyaemia. The abscess commonly occurs in the retrocaecal region.SRB 24 · p.937, p.938
And how a pelvic abscess is drained
SRB: per rectally, or through posterior colpotomy in females.SRB 24 · p.938 Which is another reason the rectal examination is not optional.

Ileocaecal tuberculosis

Drugs first, if there is no obstruction
B&L: "When the diagnosis is clear and the patient has not yet developed obstructive symptoms, treatment with antituberculous medication is advised and may be curative."B&L 74 · p.1308 For the ulcerative form, "a course of antituberculous chemotherapy usually leads to cure, provided the pulmonary tuberculosis is adequately treated".B&L 74 · p.1308
Surgery, and its two indications
"Where obstruction is present, or the possibility of Crohn's disease or lymphoma requires clarification, ileocaecal resection is often required."B&L 74 · p.1308 So you operate either because the bowel is blocked or because you do not know what the lesion is. For the ulcerative form, surgery is "usually undertaken only in the rare event of a perforation or complete intestinal obstruction".B&L 74 · p.1308
Why the nodes matter
"There is early involvement of the regional lymph nodes, which may caseate."B&L 74 · p.1308 S Das: the nodes become matted along with the involved terminal ileum and caecum to produce the lump.Das 35 · p.533 So the mass you feel is as much nodal as intestinal, which is why it is fixed and why it can be sampled by a guided biopsy.

Carcinoma of the caecum

Right hemicolectomy
B&L, on a right-sided lesion presenting acutely: "If the lesion is right sided, it is usually possible to perform a right hemicolectomy and anastomosis in the usual manner." If there has been perforation with substantial contamination, or the patient is unstable, it may be advisable to bring out an ileocolostomy rather than anastomose.B&L 77 · p.1363 That is a cleaner answer than most candidates give: the right colon can usually be anastomosed even in an emergency, unlike the left.
Where caecal cancer sits in the overall picture
Most large bowel cancers arise on the left, rectum 38%, sigmoid 21%, descending colon 4%; caecum 12% and ascending colon 5% are less common.B&L 77 · p.1359 So a caecal cancer is only about one in eight colorectal cancers, but it is a high proportion of right iliac fossa masses.
And what to protect at operation
"The important posterior relations of the caecum and ascending colon are the right ureter, right gonadal vessels and duodenum and these must be protected at surgery."B&L 77 · p.1354

7Rapid-fire viva

Give the differential diagnosis of a right iliac fossa mass.

The five common ones first: appendicular mass or abscess, carcinoma caecum, ileocaecal tuberculosis, lymph node mass and amoeboma.SRB 16 · p.723 Then Crohn's disease, actinomycosis, psoas abscess, bony swellings of the ilium, iliac artery aneurysm, an ectopic or unascended kidney, an abdominal undescended testis, and gynaecological masses, ovarian cyst, tubo-ovarian mass and pedunculated fibroid. And do not forget parietal swellings: lipoma, desmoid, fatty hernia of the linea alba and a parietal wall abscess.Das 35 · p.533 Bailey and Love's own list for a right iliac fossa mass is appendix mass, lymphoma, carcinoma of the caecum, Crohn's, tuberculosis and actinomycosis.B&L 74 · p.1308

How do you tell a parietal from an intra-abdominal mass?

The leg-lifting or head-raising test: ask the supine patient to lift both legs with knees extended, or to raise the head and shoulders with arms folded, which makes the abdominal muscles taut.Das 35 · p.520 An intra-abdominal mass becomes less prominent; a parietal mass persists unchanged but becomes less mobile.SRB 16 · p.723 Also, pitting of the skin on pressure suggests a parietal abscess, though a faecally loaded colon can also pit.Das 35 · p.519

Distinguish an appendicular mass from an appendicular abscess.

The mass contains no pus; the abscess does.Das 35 · p.533 The mass is firm with distinct borders and resonant on percussion; the abscess is soft with indistinct borders and dull.SRB 16 · p.723 The abscess has variable pyrexia and a rising leucocyte count with polymorphonuclear leucocytosis, and tends to approach the surface so that redness and oedema of the abdominal wall appear.Das 35 · p.533 The distinction matters because the abscess needs drainage, preferably radiological, and the mass does not.B&L 76 · p.1343

What is an appendicular mass made of, and why does it form?

Inflamed appendix, greater omentum, oedematous caecal wall and coils of small intestine matted together with lymph; SRB's figure names dilated ileum, greater omentum, inflamed appendix and caecum. It forms because it is an attempt by nature to wall off the inflamed appendix and prevent general peritonitis even if the appendix perforates.Das 35 · p.533SRB 24 · p.937 It appears on the third day or earlier and coincides with the position of the appendix.

Why treat an appendicular mass conservatively?

Because the inflammatory process is already localised, and inadvertent surgery is difficult and may be dangerous: it may be impossible to find the appendix, and a faecal fistula may form.B&L 76 · p.1343 SRB puts it as nature having already localised the infection, which if now disturbed will cause a faecal fistula.SRB 24 · p.937 It works: about 90% resolve without incident, with clinical improvement usually evident within 24 to 48 hours.

Describe the Ochsner-Sherren regime.

Nil by mouth with initial nasogastric aspiration, intravenous fluids, analgesia and antibiotics, ampicillin with metronidazole and gentamicin by severity, or metronidazole with a third-generation cephalosporin. Mark the limits of the mass on the abdominal wall with a skin pencil. Record temperature and pulse 4-hourly and keep a fluid balance chart. Perform a contrast-enhanced CT, and drain an abscess radiologically if one is found. Re-examine the abdomen regularly and be prepared to operate.SRB 24 · p.937B&L 76 · p.1343

When would you abandon conservative treatment?

If the patient becomes more toxic with tachycardia and high fever, with a rising pulse rate the key early sign; if there is persistent vomiting; if the pain increases or spreads, meaning diffuse peritonitis; if the mass increases in size; or if it suppurates into an abscess.SRB 24 · p.937B&L 76 · p.1343 Bailey and Love prints three of these, SRB five. Note that suppuration means radiological drainage rather than necessarily laparotomy, whereas clinical deterioration or peritonitis is an indication for early laparotomy.

What are the contraindications to the Ochsner-Sherren regimen?

When the diagnosis is in doubt, which is the most important, because you cannot watch a mass you have not diagnosed; in a burst or gangrenous appendix; and once diffuse peritonitis has set in. SRB also lists acute appendicitis in children and the elderly,SRB 24 · p.937 but note that this concerns acute appendicitis rather than an established mass, and that Bailey and Love reports conservative antibiotic treatment of uncomplicated appendicitis in children to be short-term safe with about 90% resolution, though with recurrence up to 46%.B&L 76 · p.1343

Is interval appendicectomy necessary?

The classical answer is yes, at six weeks after a mass and three months after a drained abscess.SRB 24 · p.936, p.938 Current evidence changes the rationale rather than the practice: most patients never develop recurrent appendicitis, so prevention of recurrence is a weak indication, but rates of underlying appendiceal neoplasm are higher than expected in patients who do go on to interval surgery, particularly over the age of 40. One randomised trial was terminated early because an appendiceal tumour was found during follow-up in 29%, 12 of 41, of patients over 40 who presented with a periappendicular abscess.B&L 76 · p.1343 So in a patient over 40 I would resect or at least fully investigate; in a young patient I would discuss it.

The mass has not resolved after two weeks of conservative treatment. What now?

Re-think the diagnosis. Bailey and Love: failure of the mass to resolve should raise suspicion of a carcinoma or Crohn's disease.B&L 76 · p.1343 Add ileocaecal tuberculosis and lymphoma in our population. Investigate with contrast CT and colonoscopy with biopsy, and consider an image-guided biopsy of the mass or nodes. Remember that a caecal carcinoma can obstruct the appendiceal orifice and cause the appendicitis in the first place, which is an occasional cause in middle-aged and elderly patients.B&L 76 · p.1337

Can appendicitis be treated without an operation?

In uncomplicated appendicitis, meaning no appendicolith, no perforation and no abscess, intravenous antibiotics with bowel rest succeed initially in about 85%, but between a quarter and a third need surgery within a year. Later surgery tends to be uncomplicated, with similar complication rates and length of stay. It is reasonable in a well patient with limited signs, or one at high operative risk, provided they understand the failure rate.B&L 76 · p.1343 Appendicectomy remains the standard teaching, and neither Indian text offers antibiotics as definitive treatment.

Distinguish the two forms of intestinal tuberculosis.

Ulcerative tuberculosis develops secondary to pulmonary tuberculosis from swallowing tubercle bacilli: multiple transversely lying ulcers in the terminal ileum with thickened reddened serosa covered in tubercles, presenting with diarrhoea and weight loss, sometimes subacute obstruction, perforation or fistula. Hyperplastic tuberculosis is caused by ingestion of the organism by a patient with high resistance: infection establishes in lymphoid follicles, chronic inflammation thickens the wall and narrows the lumen, regional nodes are involved early and may caseate, and the patient presents with attacks of pain, intermittent diarrhoea and a right iliac fossa mass with vague ill health.B&L 74 · p.1308 So the patient with a mass is usually the hyperplastic, relatively resistant one, and the chest may be clear.

How do you distinguish ileocaecal tuberculosis from Crohn's disease?

Three ways. Clinically, anal complications, fissure and multiple fistulae, and fistulae in the right iliac fossa, are mainly seen in Crohn's; Bailey and Love says that unlike in Crohn's, abscess and fistula formation are uncommon in tuberculosis.Das 35 · p.533B&L 74 · p.1308 By the position of the caecum: in tuberculosis fibrosis pulls the caecum up towards the lumbar region, whereas in Crohn's the caecum stays in place.SRB 16 · p.723Das 35 · p.533 And radiologically: tuberculosis gives a long narrow constricted terminal ileum and ascending colon with a high caecum, Crohn's gives a narrow smooth terminal ileum, the string sign of Kantor, devoid of peristalsis with a normal caecum.Das 35 · p.534

Describe the barium findings in carcinoma of the caecum.

An irregular filling defect affecting the caecum with a soft tissue shadow showing the extent of the tumour, but with the terminal ileum absolutely normal.Das 35 · p.534 That sparing of the ileum is the discriminator: both tuberculosis and Crohn's narrow the terminal ileum, and the cancer does not.

Why does a right-sided colon cancer present with anaemia rather than obstruction?

Because the caecum is capacious and the stool there is still liquid, so it bleeds occultly for a long time before it blocks. S Das's clinical picture for caecal carcinoma is occult blood in the stool with anaemia and rapid loss of weight, with a hard, irregular, fixed lump at a later stage.Das 35 · p.533 Caecal cancer accounts for about 12% of large bowel cancers, against 38% for rectum.B&L 77 · p.1359

Which right iliac fossa mass is mobile?

Carcinoma of the caecum, because the caecum is on a mesentery, though mobility becomes restricted once it is adherent to psoas. Ileocaecal tuberculosis has restricted mobility. An appendicular mass or abscess, and an amoeboma, are not mobile. And none of them moves with respiration, which is itself what excludes liver, gallbladder and renal masses.SRB 16 · p.723

A right iliac fossa mass with a flexed hip. What is it?

A psoas abscess, localised, smooth, soft and non-mobile, with psoas spasm producing flexion of the hip. Examine the spine for a gibbus, tenderness, paraspinal spasm and restricted movements, because the usual cause is spinal tuberculosis with a cold abscess tracking down the psoas sheath.SRB 16 · p.723 It needs treatment of the spine, not a laparotomy.

Who is at risk of perforating an appendix?

Extremes of age, immunosuppression, diabetes mellitus, faecolith obstruction of the lumen, a free-lying pelvic appendix, and previous abdominal surgery, which limits the greater omentum's ability to wall off the spread.B&L 76 · p.1338 SRB adds laxative abuse.SRB 24 · p.936 The omental point is worth giving, because it explains the mechanism rather than just listing the association.

Where can an abscess form after appendicitis?

Retrocaecal, which is commonest, and also subcaecal, preileal, lumbar, pelvic and subphrenic; and portal pyaemia as a separate entity.SRB 24 · p.937, p.938 A pelvic abscess is drained per rectally, or through posterior colpotomy in females, which is one more reason the rectal examination is compulsory in any lower abdominal mass.SRB 16 · p.723SRB 24 · p.938

What is amoebic typhlitis, and why does it matter before an appendicectomy?

An amoebic inflammation of the caecum which, S Das says, hardly produces a lump; diarrhoea is the main manifestation, and the patient complains of pain over two places, the caecum and the sigmoid colon, with a thickened tender colon palpable. Sigmoidoscopy helps the diagnosis. It matters because in tropical countries where amoebiasis is endemic one must be very careful to exclude it before doing an appendicectomy. Its complications include rectal haemorrhage, fibrous stricture, intestinal obstruction, paracolic and ischiorectal abscess, fistula and perforation.Das 35 · p.534 An amoeboma, by contrast, is a smooth, hard, well-defined non-mobile mass with a history of dysentery.SRB 16 · p.723

How is ileocaecal tuberculosis treated?

Antituberculous chemotherapy when the diagnosis is clear and there are no obstructive symptoms, and it may be curative. Ileocaecal resection is often required where obstruction is present, or where the possibility of Crohn's disease or lymphoma needs clarification.B&L 74 · p.1308 For the ulcerative form, chemotherapy usually cures provided the pulmonary disease is adequately treated, and surgery is reserved for perforation or complete obstruction.

Would you anastomose after an emergency right hemicolectomy?

Usually yes. For a right-sided lesion it is usually possible to perform a right hemicolectomy and anastomosis in the usual manner, even in the emergency setting. If there has been perforation with substantial contamination, or the patient is unstable, it may be advisable to bring out an ileocolostomy instead. This is the contrast with a left-sided lesion, where the decision lies between a Hartmann's procedure and resection with anastomosis.B&L 77 · p.1363 At operation protect the right ureter, right gonadal vessels and duodenum.B&L 77 · p.1354

What is the Blaxland ruler test?

A flat ruler is placed on the lower abdomen just above the anterosuperior iliac spines and pressed firmly backwards. In an ovarian cyst, aortic pulsation is transmitted to the fingers through the ruler; in ascites it is not.SRB 16 · p.723 It is one way of deciding whether a lower abdominal swelling is a cyst sitting on the aorta or free fluid.

Summarise the investigations you would order.

Full blood count with differential, looking for polymorphonuclear leucocytosis in an abscess or microcytic anaemia in a carcinoma; urea, electrolytes and C-reactive protein; urinalysis and a pregnancy test in a woman of childbearing age; stool for occult blood and for amoebae. Ultrasound of abdomen and pelvis first, then contrast-enhanced CT, which answers whether there is a collection to drain, a tumour, and lymphadenopathy. Colonoscopy with biopsy once the acute episode settles, to exclude carcinoma and to sample a tuberculous or Crohn's lesion. Barium follow-through if CT is unavailable, for the three patterns that separate tuberculosis, Crohn's and carcinoma. Image-guided biopsy of the mass or nodes. And diagnostic laparoscopy, with exploratory laparotomy as the last resort.SRB 16 · p.723B&L 76 · p.1342, p.1343Das 35 · p.534
