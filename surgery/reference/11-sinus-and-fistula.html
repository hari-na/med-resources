Short case 11 · General Surgery

Sinus and Fistula

Two definitions, one list of causes, and one question that decides the whole management: why has this not healed? Answer that and the operation chooses itself.

S Das: clinical method Bailey & Love: mechanism and management SRB: exam framework

1The two definitions, exactly

Sinus
S Das: "A sinus is a blind track leading from the surface down to the tissues. There may be a cavity in the tissue which is connected to the surface through a sinus. The sinus is lined by granulation tissue which may be epithelialized."Das 5 · p.76 SRB: "It is a blind track lined by granulation tissue leading from an epithelial surface into the surrounding tissues." And the etymology, which examiners like: sinus means "hollow" or "a bay", from the Latin.SRB 1 · p.30
The operative wordBlind. A sinus has one opening.
Fistula
S Das: "It is a communication" between two surfaces. SRB: "It is an abnormal communication between the lumen of one viscus to another or the body surface or between the vessels." Etymology: fistula means "flute" or "a pipe or tube".SRB 1 · p.30Das 5 · p.76
The operative wordTwo openings, or one opening and one internal communication.
And Bailey & Love, which does carry both definitions
Not in a chapter of its own. In 28e they sit at the end of chapter 45, skin and subcutaneous tissue, immediately after ulcers, which is where to look for them.
Sinus: "A sinus is a blind-ending tract connecting a cavity lined with granulation tissue (often an abscess cavity) to an epithelial surface."B&L 45 · p.663
Fistula: "A fistula is an abnormal communication between two epithelium-lined surfaces. This communication or tract is usually lined by granulation tissue, but may become epithelialised in chronic cases."B&L 45 · p.663
Read the wording closelyBoth differences from the Indian books are set out in the amber boxes below, because both are the sort of thing an examiner picks at.
What is lined by granulation tissue, the sinus or the cavity? And which way does the track run? A live disagreement in the wording of the definition itself. S Das 5 p.76 and SRB 1 p.30 against B&L 45 p.663
S Das and SRB
Both put the granulation lining in the sinus and run the track inwards from the surface. S Das: "A sinus is a blind track leading from the surface down to the tissues. There may be a cavity in the tissue which is connected to the surface through a sinus. The sinus is lined by granulation tissue which may be epithelialized."Das 5 · p.76 SRB: "It is a blind track lined by granulation tissue leading from an epithelial surface into the surrounding tissues."SRB 1 · p.30
Bailey & Love
Puts the granulation lining in the cavity and runs the track outwards to the surface: a blind-ending tract "connecting a cavity lined with granulation tissue (often an abscess cavity) to an epithelial surface".B&L 45 · p.663 Grammatically, "lined with granulation tissue" attaches to the cavity, not to the tract.
Does it matter? Yes, in one place
It is the same lesion described from the two ends, so for naming the thing it makes no difference. It matters for why a sinus persists. B&L's wording points at the cavity as the engine: an undrained abscess cavity keeps discharging through a blind tract, and B&L lists "inadequate surgical drainage of a cavity" among the causes of an acquired sinus.B&L 45 · p.663 The Indian books' wording points at the track, and at its epithelialisation, as what has to be excised. Both are true, and a complete answer uses both: drain the cavity and excise the track.
How to answer: give SRB's sentence, because it is the compact one and the one an Indian examiner expects. "A sinus is a blind track lined by granulation tissue leading from an epithelial surface into the surrounding tissues." Then add the cavity in the next breath, since it is what earns the follow-up mark: "There is usually a cavity, often an abscess cavity, at the blind end, and it is inadequate drainage of that cavity that keeps the sinus open." You have then said everything all three books say, in two sentences, without having to adjudicate the grammar.
Is an arteriovenous fistula a fistula? B&L's own definition says no, and then its own example says yes An internal contradiction inside one paragraph of B&L 45 p.663, which SRB 1 p.30 avoids
What B&L says
The definition restricts fistulae to epithelium: "A fistula is an abnormal communication between two epithelium-lined surfaces." Four lines later the examples include arteriovenous among acquired fistulae.B&L 45 · p.663 A blood vessel is lined by endothelium, which is not epithelium, so an arteriovenous fistula fails B&L's own definition while appearing in B&L's own list. The same objection applies to a tracheo-oesophageal fistula only in part, since both of those surfaces genuinely are epithelial.
What SRB says
SRB writes the definition so that vessels are included from the start: "It is an abnormal communication between the lumen of one viscus to another or the body surface or between the vessels."SRB 1 · p.30 That extra clause, or between the vessels, is doing real work, and it is why SRB's definition survives its own examples.
Why B&L's wording is still the one you will see
"Two epithelium-lined surfaces" is the classical teaching definition and it is what distinguishes a fistula from a sinus crisply: two lined surfaces against one. The arteriovenous case is the recognised exception rather than a reason to abandon the definition.
How to answer: use SRB's wording, because it covers every example you will be asked to give. "A fistula is an abnormal communication between the lumen of one viscus and another, or the body surface, or between vessels." If you have already said "between two epithelium-lined surfaces" and the examiner asks about an arteriovenous fistula, do not retreat: say that vessels are lined by endothelium rather than epithelium, so the strict epithelial definition takes arteriovenous fistula as a recognised exception, which is why SRB words it to include vessels. Volunteering that distinction turns an apparent slip into a point.

S Das notes that some sinuses and fistulae are present since birth, for example the preauricular sinus, and that a sinus due to osteomyelitis follows a history of high fever: S Das ch.5 p.76. B&L ch.45 p.663 groups the acquired causes of a sinus by mechanism, which is a better way to recite them: a retained foreign body (an ingrown hair or suture material), chronic infection (tuberculosis, osteomyelitis or actinomycosis), chronic inflammation (Crohn's disease), malignancy, or inadequate surgical drainage of a cavity. Five headings, and the last one is the one candidates forget.

The discharge tells you the source, and this table is pure marks SRB's list, which is the single most useful thing in this case.SRB 1 · p.31

Purulent: bacterial infection.
Gaseous: tuberculous.
Sulphur granules: actinomycosis.
Mucus: branchial fistula.
Saliva: parotid fistula.
Faeces: faecal fistula.
Bile: biliary or duodenal fistula.
Bone, that is bony spicules: osteomyelitis.

So the first question at the bedside is not "where does it go" but "what comes out of it".

3Why has it not healed? The question the case turns on

Causes of persistence, and both books give almost the same list SRB's version, as a printed box:SRB 1 · p.31

1. A foreign body or necrotic tissue underneath, for example a suture or a sequestrum.
2. Insufficient or non-dependent drainage.
3. Persistent obstruction in the lumen, for example distal obstruction in a faecal or biliary fistula.
4. Lack of rest, persistent infection.
5. The wall becomes lined with epithelium or endothelium.
6. Dense fibrosis prevents contraction and healing.
7. Specific infections: tuberculosis, actinomycosis.

S Das's list is the same, and adds the mechanism for one of them: the walls may be held apart, "preventing their collapse, as occurs in chronic empyema".Das 5 · p.76 And his blunt observation about fistulae: "once a true fistula has been formed, it seldom shows any" tendency to close.Das 5 · p.76

Learn these seven. Every one of them is a thing you can do something about, which is why they are the answer to "how will you treat it?"

4Examination

The opening
Number, site, and whether the edge is everted, indurated or healthy. A single opening means a sinus; two or more raise a fistula. SRB illustrates a pilonidal sinus with primary and secondary openings.SRB 1 · p.31
The discharge
Express it and look at it, against the table above. Culture it, and in India send it for acid-fast bacilli and cytology as well, which is SRB's routine in a median mental sinus: discharge study for culture and sensitivity, cytology, and AFB.SRB 1 · p.32
The surrounding skin
Scarring, pigmentation, excoriation. SRB notes that in a postoperative gastrointestinal fistula the skin excoriation can be controlled by zinc oxide cream applied locally,SRB 1 · p.31 which is the kind of practical detail that marks out a candidate who has been on a ward.
The track
Palpate along it for induration and for a cord. Gently probe only if it is safe to do so, and remember S Das's use of the probe in a tuberculous ulcer: a probe insinuating easily under an undermined edge is diagnostic.Das 4 · p.63 Note the direction: in a preauricular sinus the track runs backwards and slightly upwards towards the helix and usually ends blindly.SRB 1 · p.33
The underlying structure
Bone, joint, bowel, or lymph nodes. SRB illustrates a typical sinus in the thigh due to osteomyelitis of the greater trochanter,SRB 1 · p.30 and a sinus in the neck due to tuberculosis of lymph nodes with a cold abscess underneath.SRB 1 · p.31 Those two sentences name the two commonest causes of a chronic sinus in Indian practice.
Exuberant granulation at the opening
Proud flesh. SRB: granulation tissue sprouting out of the sinus opening, commonly associated with a retained foreign body.SRB 1 · p.19 So it is a sign, not just an appearance.

5The named sinuses worth knowing

Preauricular sinus
Congenital, "occurring due to imperfect fusion of the six tubercles which form the ear cartilage". The opening is at the root of the helix or on the tragus; the track runs deep, backwards and slightly upwards towards the helix and usually ends blindly. If the outer opening closes, a swelling forms. Differential diagnosis: cold abscess, sebaceous cyst. Investigation: sinusogram and discharge study, with MR sinusogram beneficial. Treatment: excision under general anaesthesia with removal of the entire track, and "if the track is not removed properly recurrence will occur"; methylene blue is injected into the track first and the track excised through an elliptical incision.SRB 1 · p.33
Median mental sinus
A chronic acquired infective condition in which infection of the root or roots of one or both lower incisor teeth forms a root abscess that tracks down between the two halves of the lower jaw in the midline, presenting as a discharging sinus at the point of the chin in the midline. Usually painless.
The trapSRB: "It is often mistaken for an infected sebaceous cyst." Differential diagnosis: infected sebaceous cyst, tuberculous sinus, osteomyelitis, and osteomyelitis of the mandible is the possible complication.SRB 1 · p.32
The investigation that settles it"Dental X-ray is diagnostic. Plain X-ray mandible may not reveal the disease." Treatment: antibiotics after a discharge study, then lay open and excise the sinus track with extraction of the offending incisor tooth or teeth.SRB 1 · p.32
And the point of the caseYou cannot cure it without removing the tooth, because the tooth is the foreign body. It is cause-of-persistence number one in action.
Sequestrum, since it is the commonest foreign body
SRB: "Sequestrum is dead bone in situ." And it has characteristic appearances by cause: pyogenic; tubercular, which is feathery; Salmonella, which is granular; and syphilitic.SRB 1 · p.32 Treatment is sequestrectomy and saucerisation.SRB 1 · p.33
Pilonidal sinus
Has primary and secondary openings.SRB 1 · p.31 Note its company: SRB lists pilonidal sinus as associated with hidradenitis suppurativa, along with acne and Crohn's disease, where smoking is the important aetiological factor and the other associations are obesity, poor hygiene, polycystic ovarian disease, diabetes mellitus and steroid therapy, with a genetic association at chromosome 15q24q25.SRB 1 · p.44

6Investigations and treatment

Discharge study
Culture and sensitivity, cytology, and acid-fast bacilli.SRB 1 · p.32
Sinusogram or fistulogram
Contrast injected into the track to show its extent, any cavity, and any communication. SRB names the MR sinusogram as beneficial in a preauricular sinus.SRB 1 · p.33
Imaging of the underlying bone or viscus
Plain radiograph for a sequestrum, and dental radiograph for a median mental sinus, where a plain mandible film may miss it entirely.SRB 1 · p.32 CT or MRI where the track is deep or the anatomy complex.
Biopsy of the track
For tuberculosis, actinomycosis and malignancy. B&L states it as a rule rather than an option: "Biopsies should always be taken from the wall of a sinus to exclude malignancy or specific infection."B&L 45 · p.663 Say always if asked, and give the reason: a long-standing sinus can harbour a squamous carcinoma in its wall, and no imaging will tell you. SRB's general approach in a chronic sinus with cold abscess: send material for histology, and give antibiotics, antitubercular drugs, rest and adequate drainage.SRB 1 · p.32
Treatment, in principle
B&L reduces it to one sentence for each: "Treatment of a sinus is directed at removing the underlying cause" and "management of a fistula is directed at the underlying aetiology".B&L 45 · p.663 In full: remove the cause of persistence, then excise the track. That means: take out the foreign body or sequestrum; relieve the distal obstruction; provide dependent drainage; rest the part; treat the specific infection; and excise the epithelialised or densely fibrosed track.SRB 1 · p.31 Excise the whole track or it recurs, which SRB states of the preauricular sinus and which is true of all of them.SRB 1 · p.33

7Rapid-fire viva

Define a sinus and a fistula.

A sinus is a blind track lined by granulation tissue leading from an epithelial surface into the surrounding tissues; it has one opening, and there may be a cavity connected to the surface through it. A fistula is an abnormal communication between the lumen of one viscus and another, or the body surface, or between vessels; it has two openings or one opening and an internal communication.Das 5 · p.76SRB 1 · p.30 Sinus means hollow or a bay; fistula means a flute or pipe.

Why does a sinus persist?

Seven reasons, and each is a target for treatment: a foreign body or necrotic tissue such as a suture or sequestrum; insufficient or non-dependent drainage; persistent obstruction in the lumen, as with a distal obstruction in a faecal or biliary fistula; lack of rest and persistent infection; epithelialisation or endothelialisation of the wall; dense fibrosis preventing contraction; and specific infections such as tuberculosis and actinomycosis.SRB 1 · p.31 S Das adds the mechanism where the walls are held apart and cannot collapse, as in chronic empyema.Das 5 · p.76

What does the discharge tell you?

Purulent means bacterial infection; gaseous means tuberculous; sulphur granules mean actinomycosis; mucus means a branchial fistula; saliva means a parotid fistula; faeces a faecal fistula; bile a biliary or duodenal fistula; and bony spicules osteomyelitis.SRB 1 · p.31 So inspect and express the discharge before you probe anything.

A discharging sinus at the point of the chin. Diagnosis?

Median mental sinus: infection of the root of one or both lower incisors forms a root abscess that tracks down in the midline between the two halves of the lower jaw, presenting as a usually painless discharging sinus at the point of the chin. It is often mistaken for an infected sebaceous cyst. A dental radiograph is diagnostic, and a plain mandible film may not reveal it. Treatment is antibiotics after a discharge study, then laying open and excising the track with extraction of the offending tooth; osteomyelitis of the mandible is the possible complication.SRB 1 · p.32 The principle is that you cannot cure it without removing the tooth, because the tooth is the foreign body.

A sinus in front of the ear since childhood.

Preauricular sinus, congenital, from imperfect fusion of the six tubercles that form the ear cartilage. The opening is at the root of the helix or on the tragus, and the track runs deep, backwards and slightly upwards towards the helix, usually ending blindly. Differential diagnosis is a cold abscess or a sebaceous cyst. Investigate with a sinusogram, ideally MR, and a discharge study. Treat by excision under general anaesthesia with removal of the entire track, using methylene blue injected into the track first and an elliptical incision; incomplete removal means recurrence.SRB 1 · p.33

What is a sequestrum, and how do the varieties differ?

Dead bone in situ. Pyogenic, tubercular which is feathery, Salmonella which is granular, and syphilitic.SRB 1 · p.32 It is the commonest foreign body keeping a sinus open, and it is treated by sequestrectomy and saucerisation.SRB 1 · p.33

There is exuberant granulation tissue at the sinus opening. What does that suggest?

Proud flesh, which is commonly associated with a retained foreign body in the sinus cavity, so look for one. It should be scooped out with a Volkmann's scoop before any grafting.SRB 1 · p.19

A chronic sinus in the neck. What are you thinking?

Tuberculous lymphadenitis with a cold abscess underneath, which SRB illustrates exactly so.SRB 1 · p.31 Send the discharge for culture, cytology and acid-fast bacilli, take material for histology, and treat with antitubercular drugs, antibiotics, rest and adequate drainage.SRB 1 · p.32 The differential includes a branchial fistula, which discharges mucus, and actinomycosis, which discharges sulphur granules.

A sinus in the thigh with bony spicules in the discharge.

Chronic osteomyelitis with a sequestrum; SRB's illustration is a sinus in the thigh from osteomyelitis of the greater trochanter.SRB 1 · p.30 S Das notes the history: a sinus due to osteomyelitis follows an episode of high fever.Das 5 · p.76 Radiograph the bone, remove the sequestrum, and saucerise.

Why does a true fistula rarely close on its own?

S Das's observation is that once a true fistula has formed it seldom shows any tendency to close.Das 5 · p.76 The reasons are the causes of persistence: the track becomes lined with epithelium or endothelium, there is often distal obstruction keeping flow through it, and the flow itself prevents rest. So treatment addresses the obstruction and then the track.

How would you protect the skin around a gastrointestinal fistula?

Zinc oxide cream applied locally controls the skin excoriation.SRB 1 · p.31 And address the cause of persistence: distal obstruction, inadequate drainage, and the nutritional state.

What is pilonidal sinus associated with?

It has primary and secondary openings,SRB 1 · p.31 and SRB lists it alongside acne and Crohn's disease in association with hidradenitis suppurativa, for which smoking is the important aetiological factor, with obesity, poor hygiene, polycystic ovarian disease, diabetes and steroid therapy as other associations and a genetic link at chromosome 15q24q25.SRB 1 · p.44
