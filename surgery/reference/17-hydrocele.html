Short case 17 · General Surgery

Hydrocele

Three signs make the diagnosis: you can get above it, it transilluminates, and you cannot feel the testis separately. Then one question decides the management: is it primary, or is the testis underneath it diseased?

S Das: clinical method Bailey & Love: mechanism and management SRB: exam framework

1Definition and types

Definition
B&L: "A hydrocele is an abnormal collection of serous fluid in a part of the processus vaginalis, usually the tunica vaginalis around the testis and occasionally along the spermatic cord."B&L 86 · p.1564 SRB: the collection of fluid between the two layers of the tunica.SRB 26 · p.1057
Incidence, and the newborn figure that explains everything
Hydroceles affect an estimated 1% of adult men. More than 80% of newborn boys have a patent processus vaginalis, but most close spontaneously,B&L 86 · p.1564 with closure beginning in late gestation and continuing through infancy and childhood.B&L 86 · p.1564 That is why a congenital hydrocele in an infant is usually watched, and why one in an adult is not.
B&L's three structural types
1. Communicating or congenital: a patent processus vaginalis permits flow of peritoneal fluid into the tunica vaginalis, and it is associated with indirect inguinal hernias. 2. Non-communicating or vaginal: the processus is closed, with no communication with the peritoneal cavity; fluid accumulates from excessive production or defective absorption. 3. And the cord variety, where the collection lies along the spermatic cord.B&L 86 · p.1564
SRB's four anatomical types, which is the Indian exam answer
Vaginal hydrocele: around the testis, the commonest. Infantile hydrocele: extends up to the external ring but does not communicate with the peritoneum. Congenital hydrocele: communicates with the peritoneal cavity through a patent processus at the internal ring. Encysted hydrocele of the cord: a localised collection along the cord.SRB 26 · p.1057
How to keep them straightWork from the internal ring down: congenital reaches the peritoneum, infantile stops at the external ring, vaginal is confined to the tunica, encysted is an isolated pocket on the cord.
And primary versus secondary
B&L: acquired hydroceles are primary or idiopathic, or secondary to epididymal or testicular disease.B&L 86 · p.1564 That distinction is the whole management question.
The fluid, and the detail that explains a clinical fact SRB: hydrocele fluid is amber-coloured with a specific gravity of 1.022 to 1.024, and contains water, salts, albumin and fibrinogen. Then the sentence worth memorising: "Per se, hydrocele fluid does not clot, but if it comes in contact with the blood, fibrinogen gets activated and clots firmly." It often contains cholesterol and tyrosine crystals.SRB 26 · p.1057

So: aspirate a hydrocele and the fluid stays liquid; aspirate it traumatically and the blood in it makes it clot, which is why repeated aspiration gives you a thick-walled, loculated sac that is harder to operate on later.
Aetiology of a primary hydrocele
SRB's four mechanisms: defective absorption of fluid by the tunica vaginalis, probably from damage to the endothelial wall by low-grade infection; excessive production, as in a secondary hydrocele; interference with drainage of fluid; and a patent processus.SRB 26 · p.1057

2Examination: the three signs, and the fourth that matters most

1. Can you get above it?
Yes in a hydrocele: it is a purely scrotal swelling, and above it you feel only normal cord. No in an inguinoscrotal hernia, where the swelling is continuous with the canal.Das 38 · p.597–598 This is the first and most important manoeuvre, because it separates hydrocele from hernia before anything else.
2. Does it transilluminate?
Yes, in a darkened room with a torch applied to one side. A clear fluid-filled sac glows. It will not transilluminate if the sac is thick-walled, if the fluid is blood (haematocele) or pus (pyocele), or if the swelling is solid.
3. Is it fluctuant, and can you feel the testis?
Fluctuant, and in a vaginal hydrocele the testis is not palpable separately because the fluid surrounds it. In an epididymal cyst you can feel the testis separately, below and in front. That is the cleanest distinction between the two commonest scrotal cysts.
4. Is the testis normal? The question that matters most
Because a secondary hydrocele is secondary to epididymal or testicular disease,B&L 86 · p.1564 and the diseases in question are tuberculous epididymo-orchitis and testicular tumour. In a tense primary hydrocele you cannot palpate the testis, which is exactly why you must image it.
SoEvery hydrocele in an adult needs an ultrasound of the testis. That is the single most important sentence in this case.
Primary versus secondary, at the bedside Primary: large, often tense, testis impalpable within it, no pain, long history.
Secondary: usually small and lax, so the testis is palpable and the underlying disease can be felt; often with pain, or a history of fever, trauma or weight loss.

The rule of thumb is counter-intuitive and therefore examinable: a big tense hydrocele is usually primary and benign; a small lax hydrocele is the one hiding a tumour. And in either case, the ultrasound decides.

SRB's chapter sets the differentials out together: haematocele, pyocele, cyst of epididymis, spermatocele, varicocele, and primary and secondary vaginal hydrocele.SRB 26 · p.1056

3Investigations

Ultrasound of the scrotum
To confirm the fluid, and more importantly to see the testis you cannot palpate.B&L 86 · p.1565 Non-negotiable in an adult, and particularly in a young adult, because a testicular tumour can present as a hydrocele.
Tumour markers if the ultrasound is abnormal
Alpha-fetoprotein, beta-human chorionic gonadotrophin and lactate dehydrogenase, before any intervention.
And look for the cause of a secondary hydrocele
In India, consider filariasis, which is covered in B&L 28e's tropical infections chapter, where the further reading includes a systematic review of the surgical treatment of genital manifestations of lymphatic filariasis.B&L 6 · p.93 And consider tuberculous epididymo-orchitis, which gives a craggy, non-tender epididymis and sometimes a beaded vas.

4Treatment

In an infant
Observe. More than 80% of newborn boys have a patent processus vaginalis but most close spontaneously,B&L 86 · p.1564 so a congenital hydrocele usually resolves. If it persists beyond about two years, or if there is a hernia, operate: herniotomy with ligation of the patent processus, approached through the groin rather than the scrotum.
In an adult: the three operations, and when each
B&L: "There are three main surgical techniques for hydroceles."B&L 86 · p.1565
1. Plication, Lord's operation, which is "suitable when the sac is reasonably thin walled". The sac is not excised or everted; it is bunched up with a series of sutures, so there is minimal dissection and little haematoma.B&L 86 · p.1565
2. Eversion, Jaboulay's procedure, placing the testis in a pouch prepared by dissection in the fascial planes of the scrotum.B&L 86 · p.1565 Used for a thicker sac.
3. Excision of the sac, for a thick-walled or calcified sac, which is the most radical and the most prone to haematoma.
The ruleThin sac, plicate (Lord's). Thick sac, evert (Jaboulay's) or excise.B&L 86 · p.1565 Being able to say what decides between them, the thickness of the sac, is worth more than the eponyms.
Aspiration
Relieves the symptom and recurs, and the fluid will clot if blood enters it.SRB 26 · p.1057 So it has a place only as palliation in a patient unfit for surgery, or to confirm the diagnosis where ultrasound is unavailable, never as definitive treatment. And never aspirate without first excluding a tumour.
For a secondary hydrocele
Treat the cause. Draining the fluid without addressing a tuberculous epididymitis or a tumour achieves nothing and, in the case of a tumour, is harmful.

5Rapid-fire viva

Define a hydrocele and give its types.

An abnormal collection of serous fluid in part of the processus vaginalis, usually the tunica vaginalis around the testis and occasionally along the spermatic cord.B&L 86 · p.1564 Structurally: communicating or congenital, where a patent processus lets peritoneal fluid into the scrotum and which is associated with indirect inguinal hernia; and non-communicating or vaginal, where the processus is closed and fluid accumulates from excessive production or defective absorption. Anatomically SRB gives four: vaginal, infantile reaching the external ring, congenital reaching the peritoneum at the internal ring, and encysted hydrocele of the cord.SRB 26 · p.1057 And clinically, primary or idiopathic versus secondary to epididymal or testicular disease.

How do you distinguish a hydrocele from an inguinoscrotal hernia?

You can get above a hydrocele, feeling only normal cord above it, because it is a purely scrotal swelling; you cannot get above an inguinoscrotal hernia, which is continuous with the inguinal canal and has no upper limit.Das 38 · p.597–598 A hydrocele also transilluminates and has no cough impulse and is not reducible.

Why must every adult hydrocele have an ultrasound?

Because a hydrocele may be secondary to epididymal or testicular disease,B&L 86 · p.1564 and in a tense hydrocele you cannot palpate the testis to exclude a tumour. A testicular tumour can present as a hydrocele, so the ultrasound is examining the testis, not the fluid. If the testis is abnormal, take tumour markers before any intervention.

Which is more likely to be hiding a tumour: a big tense hydrocele or a small lax one?

The small lax one. A large tense primary hydrocele is usually idiopathic; a secondary hydrocele is typically small and lax, so the testis remains palpable and the underlying disease can be felt. Either way the ultrasound decides, because palpation of a testis through fluid is unreliable.

Describe the hydrocele fluid.

Amber-coloured, specific gravity 1.022 to 1.024, containing water, salts, albumin and fibrinogen, and often cholesterol and tyrosine crystals. It does not clot by itself, but if blood enters it the fibrinogen is activated and it clots firmly.SRB 26 · p.1057 That is why repeated or traumatic aspiration leaves a thickened loculated sac that is harder to operate on later.

Why do most congenital hydroceles need no operation?

Because more than 80% of newborn boys have a patent processus vaginalis and most close spontaneously, closure beginning in late gestation and continuing through infancy and childhood.B&L 86 · p.1564 So observe in infancy, and operate if it persists beyond about two years or if there is an associated hernia, through the groin with ligation of the patent processus.

What are the operations for an adult hydrocele, and what decides between them?

Three. Plication, Lord's operation, suitable when the sac is reasonably thin walled, bunching the sac with sutures and minimal dissection. Eversion, Jaboulay's procedure, placing the testis in a pouch prepared by dissection in the fascial planes of the scrotum. And excision of the sac, for a thick-walled or calcified sac.B&L 86 · p.1565 The thickness of the sac decides: thin sac, plicate; thick sac, evert or excise. Excision carries the highest risk of haematoma.

Would you aspirate a hydrocele?

Not as definitive treatment: it recurs, and the fluid clots if blood enters it, leaving a thickened sac.SRB 26 · p.1057 It has a place as palliation in a patient unfit for surgery. And never aspirate before excluding a testicular tumour on ultrasound, because a scrotal needle or incision in the presence of a tumour risks local spread and changes the surgical approach.

Hydrocele or epididymal cyst?

In a vaginal hydrocele the fluid surrounds the testis so the testis cannot be felt separately; in an epididymal cyst the testis is palpable separately, below and in front of the cyst. Both transilluminate and both are fluctuant and both allow you to get above them. SRB groups the scrotal differentials together: haematocele, pyocele, cyst of epididymis, spermatocele and varicocele, alongside primary and secondary vaginal hydrocele.SRB 26 · p.1056

What causes a secondary hydrocele?

Epididymal or testicular disease.B&L 86 · p.1564 In practice: acute or tuberculous epididymo-orchitis, testicular tumour, trauma, torsion and filariasis. In India filariasis is important, and the surgical treatment of genital manifestations of lymphatic filariasis appears in Bailey and Love's tropical infections chapter.B&L 6 · p.93 Treat the cause, not just the fluid.

What happens if a hydrocele does not transilluminate?

Reconsider the diagnosis. Failure to transilluminate suggests a thick-walled or calcified sac, blood (haematocele), pus (pyocele), or a solid swelling such as a tumour or a chronic inflammatory mass. Get an ultrasound.
