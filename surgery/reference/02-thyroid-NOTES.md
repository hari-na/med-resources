## The nine conflicts to build expanders around

1. **Four or five cardinal signs of primary toxic goitre?** S Das p.383 text says five (eye signs,
   tachycardia, tremor, moist skin, bruit); its own Fig. 27.26 on p.384 says four and lists a
   different set (exophthalmos, thyroid swelling with thrill, tachycardia, tremor). The book
   contradicts itself.
2. **Kocher's test vs Kocher's sign.** S Das p.380: compressing the lateral lobes produces
   stridor. SRB p.462: an eye sign, the upper lid springs up faster than the eyebrow. Same
   eponym, unrelated things.
3. **Crile's method vs Crile's grading.** S Das p.379: palpation with the thumb while the patient
   swallows. SRB p.462: sleeping pulse averaged over three consecutive nights.
4. **Which function test first?** S Das p.386 teaches PBI and T3 resin uptake (obsolete);
   SRB p.449 calls free T3 the best single test; B&L p.751 says TSH, with T3/T4 only if TSH is
   abnormal.
5. **Subtotal or total thyroidectomy for Graves?** Classical teaching is subtotal; SRB p.465
   concedes total may be better; B&L Table 51.6 p.753 lays out the trade-off explicitly.
6. **Total thyroidectomy for low-risk papillary cancer?** SRB favours near-total/total;
   B&L p.766 says guidelines advocate it but it is unlikely to improve on 99% survival and
   "many patients will be overtreated".
7. **Carbimazole dosing.** SRB p.464 gives both 5–10 mg 8-hourly and 20 mg tds in the same
   chapter; B&L p.758 gives 10 mg tds–qds then 5 mg bd–tds.
8. **Complication rates.** SRB p.483: RLN palsy 3% transient, hypoparathyroidism 0.5%.
   B&L p.762: BAES audit 1.8% → 0.5%, permanent hypoparathyroidism <1%; Table 51.6 gives 5% vs
   1% for total vs subtotal.
9. **The eponyms themselves.** Bailey & Love contains **none** of Pemberton, Lahey, Kocher,
   Crile, Pizzillo, Berry or Dalrymple. The eponyms Indian examiners ask for live in S Das and
   SRB only. Worth saying out loud so nobody wastes time hunting for them in B&L.
